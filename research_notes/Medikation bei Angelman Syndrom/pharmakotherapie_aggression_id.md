## 1. Antipsychotics (risperidone, haloperidol, aripiprazole, zuclopenthixol, pipamperone, melperone, quetiapine, olanzapine, lurasidone/ziprasidone): evidence, dosing, adverse effects, German approval status

### Takeaway
Evidence that antipsychotics beat placebo for aggression in **adults** with ID is weak and contradictory. The largest independent adult RCT (Tyrer 2008, NACHBID) found placebo **at least as good** as risperidone or haloperidol. An industry-funded RCT (Gagiano 2005) found risperidone better than placebo over 4 weeks. Cochrane found no RCT-based evidence of benefit or harm. In Germany, adult use of risperidone for ID-related aggression is **off-label**. Oral zuclopenthixol (Ciatyl-Z) is the one antipsychotic found with an explicit label for "Erregungszustände bei geistiger Behinderung". Melperone and pipamperone are labelled for non-specific "psychomotorische Unruhe/Erregungszustände" and sleep disorders, with no ID-specific controlled evidence. For an already obese man with sugar craving, olanzapine (and clozapine) carry the worst metabolic profile. Aripiprazole, lurasidone and ziprasidone are the most metabolically favourable in schizophrenia data.

### Cited Findings

**Risperidone and haloperidol: Tyrer et al. 2008 (NACHBID), adults with ID**
- Citation: Tyrer P, et al. "Risperidone, haloperidol, and placebo in the treatment of aggressive challenging behaviour in patients with intellectual disability: a randomised controlled trial." Lancet 2008;371:57–63 (editorial by Matson & Wilkins, same issue, pp. 9–10). Trial registration ISRCTN11736448. — [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2008/1101/p1090.html); [ISRCTN11736448](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN11736448); [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/neuroleptics-in-the-treatment-of-aggressive-challenging-behaviour/)
- 86 non-psychotic adults with ID and aggressive challenging behaviour were recruited by 22 clinicians and randomised to haloperidol, risperidone or placebo. Starting doses were haloperidol 2.5 mg/day and risperidone 1.0 mg/day, flexibly increased. Mean final daily doses were about 1.78 mg risperidone and 2.94 mg haloperidol. — [PharmaTimes](https://pharmatimes.com/news/lancet_study_belies_antipsychotic_effect_in_intellectually_impaired_986066/); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/93001/)
- At 4 weeks aggression fell in all arms. The largest reduction from baseline was with **placebo (79%)**, then haloperidol (65%) and risperidone (58%). Differences were not significant. The authors concluded that antipsychotics "should no longer be regarded as an acceptable routine treatment for aggressive challenging behaviour" in ID. — [PharmaTimes](https://pharmatimes.com/news/lancet_study_belies_antipsychotic_effect_in_intellectually_impaired_986066/)
- Mean 6-month costs were £16,336 (placebo), £17,626 (haloperidol) and £18,954 (risperidone). Only 3 patients withdrew for adverse effects (1 haloperidol, 2 risperidone). Retention was 71% at 12 weeks and 57% at 26 weeks. — [PharmaTimes](https://pharmatimes.com/news/lancet_study_belies_antipsychotic_effect_in_intellectually_impaired_986066/)

**Risperidone: Gagiano et al. 2005, adults with ID (industry-sponsored)**
- Citation: Gagiano C, Read S, Thorpe L, Eerdekens M, Van Hove I. "Short- and long-term efficacy and safety of risperidone in adults with disruptive behavior disorders." Psychopharmacology (Berl) 2005;179:629–636. DOI 10.1007/s00213-004-2093-2. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-004-2093-2); [Huddersfield eprint](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/668/)
- 4-week double-blind RCT: risperidone flexible 1–4 mg/day (n=39) vs placebo (n=38). Mean modal dose was **1.8 mg/day**. Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC) improved by −27.3 points (52.8%) on risperidone vs −14.9 (31.3%) on placebo, which was significant. A 48-week open-label extension followed. The authors concluded risperidone was "effective and well tolerated". — [Huddersfield eprint](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/668/)
- Note: the search snippet describes the sample as "intellectually disabled adults". Whether borderline IQ was included could not be confirmed from the abstract. — [Huddersfield eprint](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/668/)

**Cochrane review (antipsychotics, challenging behaviour, ID)**
- Brylewski J, Duggan L. "Antipsychotic medication for challenging behaviour in people with learning disability." Cochrane Database Syst Rev, CD000377 (first 2001, revised 2004, edited 2009 with no change to conclusions). DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD000377.pub2. Nine RCTs were included. Conclusion: no RCT-based evidence on whether antipsychotics help or harm adults with learning disability and challenging behaviour, and little evidence that benefits outweigh risks. Authors declared honoraria from Janssen and other companies. No newer Cochrane update was found. — [Cochrane summary CD000377](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000377_antipsychotic-medication-challenging-behaviour-people-learning-disability); [Cochrane Library record](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000377.pub2/information)

**Children with ID: meta-analysis (for context; do not transfer directly to adults)**
- McQuire C, et al. "Pharmacological interventions for challenging behaviour in children with intellectual disabilities: a systematic review and meta-analysis." BMC Psychiatry 2015;15:303. DOI 10.1186/s12888-015-0688-2. 14 RCTs, 912 children. Short-term reduction of challenging behaviour: **risperidone SMD −1.09**, **aripiprazole SMD −0.64** (both p<0.001). Significant adverse effects: **prolactin SMD 3.22** and **weight gain SMD 0.82**. Studies were mostly low quality, with no long-term follow-up. — [BMC Psychiatry](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-015-0688-2)
- A newer (~2025) BJPsych Open meta-analysis of pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions in children with ID "found no significant effects of pharmacological interventions in reducing behaviours that challenge". — [BJPsych Open](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/behaviours-that-challenge-in-children-with-intellectual-disability-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-of-pharmacological-and-nonpharmacological-interventions/86D4DB406EA669D45A715CBC50F13D7D)
- Search snippet note: a 2025 Frontiers in Psychiatry systematic review (Pascucci et al.) of antipsychotics in ID found mixed RCT results. Some trials showed reduced aggression or irritability with risperidone or olanzapine; others showed no advantage over placebo. Sedation, weight gain and metabolic changes were consistently reported. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025, 10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1609408](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1609408/text)

**Risperidone: German approval (Fachinformation)**
- Risperidone (e.g. Risperidon dura, Fachinformation dated June 2026) is indicated for "symptomatische Kurzzeitbehandlung (bis zu 6 Wochen) von anhaltender Aggression bei Verhaltensstörung bei Kindern im Alter ab 5 Jahren und Jugendlichen mit unterdurchschnittlicher intellektueller Funktion oder mentaler Retardierung" (DSM-IV diagnosed), when the severity of aggression or other disruptive behaviour requires drug treatment. The label states: "Wie bei allen symptomatischen Behandlungen, muss die kontinuierliche Anwendung … fortwährend beurteilt und begründet werden." — [Fachinfo Risperidon dura (fachinfo.de)](https://www.fachinfo.de/fi/detail/022710/risperidon-dura-1-mg-ml-loesung-zum-einnehmen); [PDF](https://www.fachinfo.de/pdf/022710)
- It is also indicated for short-term treatment (up to 6 weeks) of persistent aggression in moderate to severe Alzheimer's dementia unresponsive to non-pharmacological methods, and for schizophrenia and moderate to severe manic episodes of bipolar disorder. — [Fachinfo Risperidon dura](https://www.fachinfo.de/fi/detail/022710/risperidon-dura-1-mg-ml-loesung-zum-einnehmen); [Pharmazeutische Zeitung, Steckbrief Risperidon](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/steckbrief-risperidon-133657/)
- Paediatric conduct-disorder dosing per PZ: the optimal dose for most children under 50 kg is 0.5 mg once daily, and 1 mg once daily for ≥50 kg. — [Pharmazeutische Zeitung](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/steckbrief-risperidon-133657/)
- **So treating aggression in a 26-year-old adult with ID is off-label in Germany** (the ID-aggression label is restricted to children ≥5 years and adolescents). — [Fachinfo Risperidon dura](https://www.fachinfo.de/fi/detail/022710/risperidon-dura-1-mg-ml-loesung-zum-einnehmen)
- NHS England likewise calls antipsychotic use for challenging behaviour "an unlicensed indication". — [NHS England STOMP-STAMP](https://www.england.nhs.uk/learning-disabilities/improving-health/stomp-stamp/)

**Zuclopenthixol (Ciatyl-Z): Häßler et al. 2007 and German label**
- Citation: Häßler (Haessler) F, Glaser T, Beneke M, Pap AF, Bodenschatz R, Reis O. "Zuclopenthixol in adults with intellectual disabilities and aggressive behaviours: discontinuation study." Br J Psychiatry 2007;190:447–448. DOI 10.1192/bjp.bp.105.016535. — [Cambridge PDF](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/88773ABF6A182A7F579BBDE108E1827A/S0007125000172204a.pdf/zuclopenthixol_in_adults_with_intellectual_disabilities_and_aggressive_behaviours.pdf); [catalogue record with DOI](https://katalog.hszg.de/Record/ai-49-aHR0cDovL2R4LmRvaS5vcmcvMTAuMTE5Mi9ianAuYnAuMTA1LjAxNjUzNQ)
- Design: 49 adults aged 18–50 with mild to moderate ID (IQ 30–70) got 6 weeks of open zuclopenthixol for exacerbated aggression. They were then randomised to continue (n=19) or switch to placebo (n=20). The placebo group showed more aggression on the externally rated **Modified Overt Aggression Scale (MOAS)**. The authors concluded that discontinuation increases aggression and that the drug "may be indicated especially in institutional settings". Several co-authors (T.G., M.B., A.F.P.) were Bayer Vital GmbH employees (industry sponsor). — [Cambridge PDF](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/88773ABF6A182A7F579BBDE108E1827A/S0007125000172204a.pdf/zuclopenthixol_in_adults_with_intellectual_disabilities_and_aggressive_behaviours.pdf)
- Follow-up publications: a secondary-parameter analysis, a 2-year follow-up and a therapeutic drug monitoring analysis of the same trial (Pharmacopsychiatry 2014;47(01):29–32, DOI 10.1055/s-0033-1361115). — [Thieme 10.1055/s-0033-1361115](https://thieme-connect.de/products/all/doi/10.1055/s-0033-1361115); [Thieme 10.1055/s-0028-1082072](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0028-1082072)
- **German label:** the Ciatyl-Z tablet and drop Fachinformation (dated Sept 2023) lists **"Erregungszustände bei geistiger Behinderung"** as the first indication, followed by acute and chronic schizophrenia and mania. Daily dose ranges by strength include 2–6 mg/day (2 mg tablets) and 2–40 mg/day (20 mg/ml drops). — [Fachinfo Ciatyl-Z (fachinfo.de PDF 023474)](https://www.fachinfo.de/pdf/023474)
- The depot formulation's Fachinformation (May 2021) says therapeutic success should be monitored in long-term treatment to check whether the maintenance dose can be reduced. Acuphase is labelled only for initial treatment of acute psychoses. — [Fachinfo (fachinfo.de PDF 023473)](https://www.fachinfo.de/pdf/023473); [Fachinfo (PDF 023771)](https://www.fachinfo.de/pdf/023771)

**Pipamperone (Dipiperon) and melperone: German labels and evidence**
- Pipamperone Fachinformation: a low-potency neuroleptic for "Schlafstörungen, insbesondere bei geriatrischen Patienten" and "psychomotorische Erregungszustände". Dosing (oral solution): generally 2–4 mg/kg/day, up to 6 mg/kg if ineffective, divided into 3 doses. For sleep disorders a low dose (~40 mg) often suffices. Usual adult starting dose for agitation is 40 mg three times daily. A patient leaflet gives a maintenance dose of 1–3 tablets (40 mg) three times daily and a maximum of 9 tablets/day. Children <14 years start at 1 mg/kg/day. — [Fachinfo Pipamperon neuraxpharm Saft](https://www.fachinfo.de/fi/pdf/023913/pipamperon-neuraxpharm-saft); [Fachinfo PDF 021396](https://fachinfo.de/pdf/021396); [Apotheken Umschau leaflet Dipiperon](https://www.apotheken-umschau.de/medikamente/beipackzettel/dipiperon-tabletten-3097201.html)
- Pipamperone contraindications include Parkinson's disease, severe cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, severe hepatic impairment and prolactin-dependent tumours. — [DocCheck Flexikon](https://flexikon.doccheck.com/de/Pipamperon)
- Melperone Fachinformation (Melperon-neuraxpharm, Dec 2022): indicated "zur Behandlung von Schlafstörungen, Verwirrtheitszuständen und zur Dämpfung von psychomotorischer Unruhe und Erregungszuständen, insbesondere bei Patienten der Geriatrie und Psychiatrie". — [Fachinfo PDF 021380](https://fachinfo.de/pdf/021380); [Fachinfo PDF 003931](https://fachinfo.de/pdf/003931)
- Melperone can prolong the QT interval, with possible torsades de pointes, in which case treatment must be stopped. Contraindications include hypokalaemia and bradycardia. — [Shop-Apotheke product info Melperon-neuraxpharm 100 mg](https://www.shop-apotheke.com/arzneimittel/68400/melperon-neuraxpharm-100-mg.htm)
- **Melperone is a CYP2D6 inhibitor.** A 2003 study (cited in a case report) found that melperone raised venlafaxine serum levels by about 52% and raised the dextromethorphan/dextrorphan ratio. In a German pharmacovigilance analysis, melperone was the most frequently prescribed CYP inhibitor in psychiatric inpatients (n=2,504; 28.1%), and the authors urge awareness of its inhibitory potential. — [Case report, J Med Case Rep 2012](https://dx.doi.org/10.1186%2F1752-1947-6-49); [J Neural Transm pharmacovigilance study (X-MOL)](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1270501837430616064)
- **Evidence in ID:** no controlled trial of pipamperone or melperone in adults with ID was found. Pipamperone is common enough in Dutch ID care to be one of the two drugs (with risperidone) in a multicentre double-blind placebo-controlled withdrawal RCT. That trial (Erasmus MC, NTR7232; withdrawal by 25% every 4 weeks) has a published protocol, but no results were found. — [Beumer et al., BMC Psychiatry 2021 protocol](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12888-021-03437-2); [PubMed 34488701](https://www.pubmed.gov/34488701/)

**Aripiprazole**
- Deb S, Farmah BK et al. (2014 systematic review, aripiprazole for problem behaviours in ID): included studies supported efficacy but were of poor quality. Most studies reported **weight gain, increased appetite, sedation, tiredness, drooling and tremor**. Prolactin improved in some participants, and no QTc effect was seen. — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-aripiprazole-in-the-management-of-problem-be/)
- Akathisia: the only ID/ASD-specific akathisia data found came from a 2012 case series of 5 adults with ASD and challenging behaviour. Four of 5 were "much/very much improved" (CGI-I), and akathisia occurred at 30 mg in the single non-responder. No ID trial reports systematic akathisia rates. — [search summary of 2012 case series; see also Frontiers 2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1609408/text)
- German label: aripiprazole is approved for schizophrenia in adults and adolescents ≥15 years, and for manic episodes of bipolar I disorder (adolescents from 13). **It is not approved for aggression or irritability in ID or autism, so this use is off-label in Germany.** — [Fachinfo aripiprazole (fachinfo.de 021135)](https://www.fachinfo.de/pdf/021135); [Fachinfo Aripiprazol axunio](https://www.fachinfo.de/fi/detail/025930/aripiprazol-axunio-1-mg-2-5-mg-tabletten)
- In the US, risperidone and aripiprazole are FDA-approved for irritability in autistic children (stated as ages 6–17). A Utah Angelman primary-care guide says they "may be helpful" in Angelman but are "not well studied" there. — [Utah Angelman Syndrome Pediatric Primary Care Guide](https://trip.utah.edu/angelman-syndrome-pediatric-primary-care-guide/)

**Olanzapine, quetiapine, lurasidone, ziprasidone: metabolic profile**
- Pillinger T, et al. "Comparative effects of 18 antipsychotics on metabolic function in patients with schizophrenia…: a systematic review and network meta-analysis." Lancet Psychiatry 2020;7(1):64–77. DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(19)30416-X. 100 RCTs, n=25,952. **Olanzapine and clozapine had the worst metabolic profiles.** Aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lurasidone and ziprasidone were most benign. **Higher baseline weight and male sex predicted larger glucose increases.** Median trial duration was only 6 weeks. — [PubMed 31860457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31860457/); [Mental Elf commentary](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/treatment/antipsychotics/antipsychotics-metabolic-function-schizophrenia/)
- Olanzapine in ID: a 2003 retrospective open naturalistic study of 20 institutionalised adults showed reduced challenging behaviours including aggression. **Weight gain was significant**, and sedation and constipation were noted. The design cannot establish causation. — [J Clin Psychiatry 2003](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/olanzapine-self-injurious-aggressive-disruptive-behaviors/)
- Quetiapine: no dedicated trial in adults with ID and aggression was found. It appears only rarely in retrospective series (e.g. 1 of 31 trials in one review). — [search summary; Frontiers 2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1609408/text)

### Inferences
- For this patient (BMI ~30.5, male, sugar craving, constipation), olanzapine and quetiapine are poor choices: worst or high metabolic burden, male sex and higher baseline weight predict glucose worsening (Pillinger), and olanzapine caused weight gain and constipation in ID. Appetite increase on risperidone or aripiprazole could also worsen food-related aggression. That aripiprazole is "weight-neutral" is less certain in ID than in schizophrenia data (Deb & Farmah report weight gain and appetite increase).
- On-label/off-label in Germany, for adults with ID: zuclopenthixol oral (agitation in ID) is **on-label**. Melperone and pipamperone (non-specific agitation/sleep) are **on-label symptomatically**, but have no ID-specific controlled evidence and carry QT and (melperone) CYP2D6 interaction issues. Risperidone, aripiprazole, quetiapine and olanzapine for aggression in adult ID are **off-label**.
- Melperone's CYP2D6 inhibition matters if combined with CYP2D6 substrates such as risperidone, aripiprazole, atomoxetine, propranolol or many SSRIs. Levels may rise.
- The Tyrer placebo response (79%) shows that environment, attention and expectancy effects are large. That fits a learned, attention-maintained aggression pattern, where behavioural approaches act on the maintaining function.
- Typical adult ID antipsychotic doses in trials were low: risperidone about 1–2 mg/day (start 0.5–1 mg), haloperidol about 2.5–3 mg/day.

### Gaps
- Full Fachinformation texts (4.2, 4.4, 4.8) could not be fetched (fachinfo.de blocked). These are **unverified** from my background knowledge and must be checked: risperidone ADR frequencies (weight gain, somnolence, increased appetite, hyperprolactinaemia "very common/common"); haloperidol German label (I believe Haldol includes an indication for persistent aggression in children/adolescents with autism or developmental disorders, but this is unverified); whether the older melperone label (Eunerpan) listed "Oligophrenie" (searches did **not** confirm it); and the age restrictions on Ciatyl-Z for "Erregungszustände bei geistiger Behinderung" (snippet shows no paediatric restriction).

## 2. Mood stabilisers and anticonvulsants for aggression (valproate, lithium, carbamazepine, topiramate, lamotrigine, levetiracetam)

### Takeaway
Evidence is sparse and low quality. One small 1980s RCT supports lithium (Craft 1987). Valproate, carbamazepine and topiramate rest on open trials and case series. For a young man, valproate now carries EU-wide precautionary measures (paternal exposure and neurodevelopmental risk in offspring). Levetiracetam is more likely to *cause* irritability or aggression than treat it. Topiramate causes weight loss but also cognitive and language slowing, which a nonverbal patient cannot report.

### Cited Findings
- Deb S, et al. "The effectiveness of mood stabilizers and antiepileptic medication for the management of behaviour problems in adults with intellectual disability: a systematic review." J Intellect Disabil Res 2008;52:107–113. Included studies: 1 RCT (lithium), 2 non-RCTs (lithium, carbamazepine), 1 prospective uncontrolled trial (valproate), and 3 retrospective case series (lithium, valproate, topiramate). There was "some" support for lithium and some antiepileptic mood stabilisers, but significant methodological limitations. — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-mood-stabilisers-and-antiepileptic-medicatio/)
- Craft M, Ismail IA, Krishnamurti D, et al. "Lithium in the treatment of aggression in mentally handicapped patients: a double-blind trial." Br J Psychiatry 1987;150:685–689. 42 patients, 4 months, lithium vs placebo. **73% of the lithium group showed reduced aggression.** Mean weekly aggression scores and episode frequency differed significantly. Side effects occurred in **36% (lithium) vs 20% (placebo)**, mostly transient. There was no toxicity and no withdrawals. The authors concluded that lithium "merits a two-month trial" in repeated aggression not improved by better placement, occupation or company. — [Cambridge Core (BJP)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/lithium-in-the-treatment-of-aggression-in-mentally-handicapped-patients/284BB5409A31C188413C580E8DD110DA)
- Children with ID (McQuire 2015): evidence for anticonvulsants was inconclusive. Valproate and topiramate as add-ons to risperidone were analysed separately. — [BMC Psychiatry 2015](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-015-0688-2)
- A separate BJP systematic review and meta-analysis exists: "Efficacy of mood stabilisers in the treatment of impulsive or repetitive aggression" (not ID-specific). Its findings were not retrieved. — [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1A0004046907FD2058D4C85C6644BFB4/S0007125000254093a.pdf/efficacy_of_mood_stabilisers_in_the_treatment_of_impulsive_or_repetitive_aggression_systematic_review_and_metaanalysis.pdf)
- **Valproate in men (EMA/CMDh, 24 Jan 2024):** a Nordic registry study found a pooled HR of 1.50 (95% CI 1.09–2.07) for neurodevelopmental disorders in children of fathers on valproate in the 3 months before conception, vs lamotrigine or levetiracetam. Adjusted cumulative risk was about 5% vs about 3%, and causality is not confirmed. Measures: valproate should be started and supervised by a specialist in epilepsy, bipolar disorder or migraine, with regular review. Men should use effective contraception (self and partner) during treatment and for ≥3 months after stopping, and must not donate sperm. — [EMA news](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/precautionary-measures-address-potential-risk-neurodevelopmental-disorders-children-born-men-treated-valproate-medicines); [AIFA copy of EMA communication](https://www.aifa.gov.it/documents/20142/2209305/2024.01.12_com-EMA_valproato_EN.pdf)
- **Levetiracetam and perampanel** (and to a lesser degree brivaracetam) are most associated with psychobehavioural adverse events, including aggressiveness and irritability. **Topiramate** (and zonisamide) is associated with language impairment, cognitive dulling and memory problems. — [CNS Drugs 2022, DOI 10.1007/s40263-022-00955-9](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40263-022-00955-9)
- Topiramate's characteristic adverse effects are weight loss, cognitive slowing and nephrolithiasis. — [Empendium McMaster textbook table](https://empendium.com/mcmtextbook/table/031_1348)
- In ID (epilepsy context), a conference abstract reported that topiramate was well tolerated with slow titration and that no obvious everyday-relevant cognitive effects were noted. This is low-level evidence. — [AES abstract](https://aesnet.org/abstractslisting/use-of-topiramate-for-refractory-epilepsy-in-adults-and-children-with-intellectual-disability)
- Levetiracetam in adults with ID: adverse reactions were mainly mild and transient, and 8% withdrew (epilepsy indication). — [AES abstract](https://aesnet.org/abstractslisting/efficacy-and-tolerability-of-levetiracetam-in-adult-patients-with-mental-retardation)

### Inferences
- Lithium has the best (still weak, 1987) controlled ID evidence among mood stabilisers. It needs blood-level monitoring and reliable fluid intake. Its practicality in a group home depends on caregiver reliability.
- Valproate: male reproductive restrictions apply now. Valproate is also known for weight gain (see Gaps), which argues against it in an obese man.
- Topiramate's weight loss is attractive for obesity and sugar craving. But cognitive and language slowing cannot be self-reported by a nonverbal patient, so a carefully monitored trial would be needed. Evidence for topiramate against aggression in ID is case-series level only.
- Levetiracetam should not be chosen for behaviour, and should be avoided where possible if an anticonvulsant is ever needed.

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved for lamotrigine in ID aggression, carbamazepine efficacy details, or valproate/lithium weight-gain magnitudes. From background knowledge (**unverified**): valproate and lithium commonly cause weight gain; carbamazepine induces CYP3A4 and lowers levels of risperidone, aripiprazole, quetiapine etc.
- No source verified for the German approval status of lithium, valproate or carbamazepine for aggression. From background knowledge (unverified), none is approved in Germany for aggression in ID, so use would be off-label.
- No Angelman-specific data on anticonvulsants for behaviour. Note that UPD Angelman has lower epilepsy prevalence (see section 9).

## 3. Beta-blockers (propranolol), naltrexone, SSRIs and buspirone

### Takeaway
All are low-evidence options in ID. Beta-blockers have **no RCTs**. Naltrexone evidence concerns **self-injury**, not aggression, and is mixed. Antidepressants (mostly SSRIs) helped fewer than half of cases in uncontrolled studies. In Angelman syndrome, anxiety (especially separation anxiety) often shows itself as aggression, which is a rationale for treating anxiety, but it is not evidence of SSRI efficacy. No buspirone data were found.

### Cited Findings
- Ward F, Tharian P, Roy M, Deb S, Unwin GL. "Efficacy of beta blockers in the management of problem behaviours in people with intellectual disabilities: a systematic review." Res Dev Disabil 2013;34(12):4293–4303. DOI 10.1016/j.ridd.2013.08.015. Studies lent "some support" but were of poor quality, and **no RCTs** were identified. — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/efficacy-of-beta-blockers-in-the-management-of-problem-behaviours)
- Cochrane review: Rana F, Gormez A, Varghese S. "Pharmacological interventions for self-injurious behaviour in adults with intellectual disabilities." CD009084. 5 double-blind placebo-controlled trials with 50 participants in total (4 naltrexone, 1 clomipramine). The data were too limited for firm conclusions, most trials were at high risk of bias, and follow-up was short. Naltrexone results were mixed: one trial found no therapeutic effect of 100 mg single dose or 50/150 mg long-term dosing (n=26). No placebo-controlled trials existed for antipsychotics, mood stabilisers or beta-blockers for self-injury. — [Cochrane summary CD009084](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009084_drugs-treatment-self-injurious-behaviour-adults-intellectual-disabilities)
- Roy A, et al. (JIDR systematic review): some adults respond to opioid antagonists with reduced self-injury, but responders cannot be predicted. — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4YLxrBq7)
- Sohanpal SK, Deb S, Thomas C, Soni R, Lenôtre L, Unwin G. "The effectiveness of antidepressant medication in the management of behaviour problems in adults with intellectual disabilities: a systematic review." J Intellect Disabil Res 2007;51(10):750–765. Included 1 small crossover RCT, 7 prospective uncontrolled trials and 2 retrospective studies (1 clomipramine, 9 SSRIs). Aggression or self-injury improved in **fewer than half** of cases on average. The rest were unchanged or worse. — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-antidepressant-medication-in-the-management-/)
- Angelman: in a 2019 study of 50 people with AS, the most common behavioural expression of anxiety was **"aggression"**, and the most common trigger was **separation from caregiver/parent**. — [RTI publication record](https://www.rti.org/publication/anxiety-associated-separation-distress-associated-behaviours-angelman-syndrome)
- In an Angelman trial-baseline dataset, about 30% of adolescents and adults had moderate to extreme anxiety/depression problems, with an average of 5 medications per person. — [Princeton Molecular Biology publication record (HRQoL and medication use in AS)](https://molbio.princeton.edu/publications/health-related-quality-life-and-medication-use-among-individuals-angelman-syndrome)

### Inferences
- An SSRI trial is reasonable only if a clear anxiety component is identified (e.g. separation or transition-triggered aggression), not for attention-maintained or food-denial aggression. Behavioural activation and disinhibition on SSRIs is a recognised concern in ID/ASD (see Gaps).
- Propranolol could be considered for arousal-driven outbursts, but evidence is case-level only. Its contraindications (asthma, bradycardia) and the CYP2D6 interaction with melperone would need checking.

### Gaps
- No search hits for **buspirone** in ID aggression. No sourced dosing for propranolol in ID.
- I could not retrieve (search budget exhausted) the citalopram STAART trial in autistic children (King et al., Arch Gen Psychiatry 2009). From background knowledge (**unverified**): no benefit over placebo for repetitive behaviour, and more activation adverse events (impulsivity, hyperactivity, insomnia). This would support the "activation risk" point, but must be verified before citing.
- No Angelman-specific SSRI efficacy data were found. One secondary source mentions SSRIs for compulsive masturbation in adult AS males, which is not relevant here.

## 4. ADHD medication in ID (methylphenidate, guanfacine ER, clonidine, atomoxetine): evidence, adult dosing, German approval for adults

### Takeaway
Methylphenidate works in ADHD with ID, but with **smaller effects** than in typically developing children (Simonoff 2013, children with IQ 30–69: ES 0.39 parent / 0.52 teacher). Adverse effects need close monitoring. Mainstream Cochrane evidence excludes IQ<70. In Germany, **methylphenidate, atomoxetine and lisdexamfetamine are approved for adults with ADHD**. **Guanfacine ER (Intuniv) is approved only for ages 6–17**, so adult use is off-label. Clonidine is not an approved ADHD drug in Germany (unverified; see Gaps). Adult ID-specific ADHD RCTs are essentially lacking.

### Cited Findings
- Simonoff E, et al. "Randomized controlled double-blind trial of optimal dose methylphenidate in children and adolescents with severe attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and intellectual disability." J Child Psychol Psychiatry 2013;54(5):527–535. PMID 22676856. ISRCTN68384912. 122 drug-free children aged 7–15 with hyperkinetic disorder and **IQ 30–69**. Titration of ≥1 week each at 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 mg/kg/day. MPH beat placebo with **effect sizes 0.39 (parent) and 0.52 (teacher)** on the Conners ADHD index. IQ and autistic symptoms did not moderate efficacy. Typical MPH adverse effects were seen, and "medication use may require close monitoring in this vulnerable group". — [ISRCTN68384912](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN68384912); [Zendy record, DOI 10.1111/jcpp.12097](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/jcpp.12097)
- Miller J, Perera B, Shankar R. "Clinical guidance on pharmacotherapy for the treatment of ADHD in people with intellectual disability" (Expert Opin Pharmacother 2020): over 20 years, 7 RCTs of ADHD pharmacotherapy in ID (5 MPH), generally underpowered. Methylphenidate and atomoxetine, particularly at higher doses, showed "clear benefits". Evidence for clonidine and guanfacine is "limited, though promising". — [Informa/Taylor & Francis, DOI 10.1080/14656566.2020.1790524](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/14656566.2020.1790524?scroll=top); [Plymouth research portal](https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/clinical-guidance-on-pharmacotherapy-for-the-treatment-of-attenti)
- A systematic review of all RCTs of MPH in ID for core ADHD symptoms (2018) exists. Its findings were not retrieved. — [PubMed 30266025](https://www.pubmed.gov/30266025/)
- The Cochrane MPH review (Storebø et al. 2015, CD009885) required ≥75% of participants to have IQ ≥70, so it does **not** cover ID. Evidence was rated very low quality. — [Cochrane Library](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD009885.pub2/information)
- **Guanfacine (Intuniv):** the EMA indication is ADHD in children and adolescents aged 6–17 when stimulants are unsuitable, not tolerated or ineffective. In Germany it may be used only as monotherapy. — [EMA EPAR summary (German)](https://www.ema.europa.eu/de/documents/overview/intuniv-epar-summary-public_de.pdf); [Pharmazeutische Zeitung 2018](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/ausgabe-042018/pharmacon-schladming-2018/junge-und-alte-zappelphilippe/); [AkdÄ on guanfacine](https://www.akdae.de/Arzneimitteltherapie/WA/Archiv/Guanfacin.pdf)
- **Adults in Germany:** "Für die Behandlung von Erwachsenen sind bisher die Wirkstoffe MPH, Atomoxetin und Lisdexamfetamin zugelassen". MPH is the drug of first choice for children and adults. — [KV Hamburg, Wirkstoffziele ADHS, Sept 2024](https://kvhh.net/_Resources/Persistent/6/9/8/e/698e0b62d8e69c488c368662ffa767b1793aeac0/17.2_Wirkstoffziele_ADHS_September2024_AB.pdf)
- The German S3 guideline on ADHD (DGPPN/DGKJP, 2018 long version) is the reference for ADHD across the lifespan. — [DGPPN S3 ADHS Langfassung](https://www.dgppn.de/_Resources/Persistent/6f514fd31d75221054f1d880bda9637728e2b92d/Langfassung%20ADHS%20Leitlinie_080618.pdf)
- Angelman: clonidine is commonly used for **sleep** in AS. A search-engine summary reported a 2020 study of 50 referred individuals with AS: 72% had taken a sleep medication, most often melatonin, clonidine and trazodone, and most continued ≥6 months. **The primary paper URL was not identified, so this needs verification before citing.** The search hit came alongside the Dutch adult AS cohort (den Besten et al. 2021), which is a *different* paper. — [den Besten et al. 2021 (context only)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7756639/)

### Inferences
- "ADHD-like restlessness" in AS is part of the syndrome's behavioural phenotype (hyperactivity, short attention). A formal adult ADHD diagnosis is needed for on-label MPH or atomoxetine prescribing in Germany. Otherwise use is off-label.
- MPH may suppress appetite, which could help with weight but might also increase irritability. Effects in ID are smaller and adverse effects more frequent, so careful titration with structured ratings is needed.
- Alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine, clonidine) target hyperarousal and sleep and are weight-neutral to modestly sedating. In adults both are off-label for this purpose in Germany. Blood pressure and pulse need monitoring, and abrupt withdrawal causes rebound hypertension (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve (search budget exhausted) the guanfacine ER RCT in autistic children with hyperactivity (Scahill et al., Am J Psychiatry 2015). From background knowledge (**unverified**): large reduction in hyperactivity vs placebo, with drowsiness, fatigue and decreased appetite.
- **Unverified (background knowledge):** clonidine has no German ADHD label (only hypertension and other indications). The atomoxetine adult dose is typically 40 mg → 80–100 mg/day. MPH adult products (e.g. Medikinet adult, Ritalin Adult) are titrated up to ~1 mg/kg/day, max 80 mg (varies by product). Guanfacine and clonidine can cause rebound hypertension on abrupt stop. Verify all of these in the current Fachinformationen.
- No adult-ID-specific ADHD RCT was found.

## 5. Benzodiazepines: why to avoid long-term; role as acute/PRN only

### Takeaway
Paradoxical disinhibition and aggression on benzodiazepines are clinically recognised in ID and autism, but evidence is case-report or small-series level, and prevalence is unknown. Benzodiazepines are nevertheless widely used in ID. Long-term use risks tolerance, dependence, sedation and falls. Their role is limited to short-term or emergency use.

### Cited Findings
- Only a few studies address benzodiazepine adverse effects in adults with ID: a PubMed search (March 2020) found 4 studies from the prior 10 years. In a large cohort, **over 40% of 20,316 people with ID received benzodiazepines within 2 years**. A single-case study (275 diary days, mild ID) found reduced mood stability and more distressed behaviour on benzodiazepines. — [Elbing et al., case report (PMC10916351)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10916351/)
- Paton C. "Benzodiazepines and disinhibition: a review" (Psychiatric Bulletin 2002): paradoxical reactions occur in a minority. Case reports suggest they are common, but large studies and systematic reviews have not confirmed this. — [Cambridge PDF](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/421AF197362B55EDF004700452BF3BC6/S0955603600001240a.pdf/benzodiazepines-and-disinhibition-a-review.pdf)
- Learning disability and autism are listed as risk factors for paradoxical reactions (secondary reference source). — [PsychDB: Paradoxical reactions](https://www.psychdb.com/meds/paradoxical-reactions)
- In 7 autistic children, a single IM dose of diazepam was followed by more anxiety and explosive aggression (1987 report, cited in the search summary). A mechanistic hypothesis links paradoxical GABA responses to bumetanide response, based on a single case. — [UMC Utrecht record](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/paradoxical-benzodiazepine-response-a-rationale-for-bumetanide-in/)

### Inferences
- In Angelman syndrome caused by **UPD**, the GABA-A receptor subunit genes in 15q11–q13 are present in two paternal copies (unlike in deletion), so deletion-specific GABRB3 arguments do not apply. No AS-specific benzodiazepine-response data were found.
- Any PRN benzodiazepine should be one documented, limited-dose rescue plan (e.g. for acute danger), not regular use. In a group home, repeated sedating PRN use may count legally as a *freiheitsentziehende Maßnahme* (see section 10).

### Gaps
- No controlled data on benzodiazepine prevalence of paradoxical reactions in ID.
- No sourced German guidance on PRN benzodiazepines in ID.

## 6. Guidelines: NICE NG11 / NG54, STOMP, deprescribing evidence, WPA international guide, German guidelines and consensus

### Takeaway
NICE NG11 (2015) allows antipsychotics for behaviour that challenges only alongside psychological interventions, when these alone have not worked within an agreed time and the risk is very severe. Its rule: **review at 3–4 weeks, stop if no response at 6 weeks**, then a full multidisciplinary review at 3 months and at least every 6 months. STOMP (NHS England, 2016) targets over-medication. UK data show that 71% of adults with ID prescribed antipsychotics had no record of severe mental illness. Deprescribing trials show that long-term risperidone can often be withdrawn without worsening irritability, with metabolic benefits. Zuclopenthixol withdrawal increased aggression in one trial. In Germany there is **no adult-specific AWMF guideline on problem behaviour in ID**. The AWMF S2k "Intelligenzminderung" (028-042, DGKJP-led, 2021) is mainly for children and adolescents, and its validity ended on 2 June 2026. The WPA international guide (Deb 2009) exists in German as DGSGB Materialien Band 26 (Seidel 2012).

### Cited Findings

**NICE NG11 (2015), "Challenging behaviour and learning disabilities: prevention and interventions for people with learning disabilities whose behaviour challenges"**
- Consider antipsychotic medication only if psychological or other interventions alone do not produce change within an agreed time, or treatment for coexisting conditions has not helped, **and** the risk to the person or others is very severe. Use it in combination with psychological or other interventions. — [NICE NG11 recommendations (via search snippet)](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11/chapter/Recommendations); [National Health Executive](https://www.nationalhealthexecutive.com/News/medication-should-not-be-first-resort-to-challenging-behaviour--nice-/108011)
- NG11 wording (snippet): "review the effectiveness and any side effects of the medication after 3–4 weeks; stop the medication if there is no indication of a response at 6 weeks". If it is effective, "conduct a full multidisciplinary review after 3 months and then at least every 6 months covering all prescribed medication (including effectiveness, side effects and plans for stopping)". — [NICE NG11 (via search snippet)](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11/chapter/Recommendations); [Kent & Medway formulary guidance, Dec 2024](https://www.kentandmedwayformulary.nhs.uk/media/5pvfu32l/pharmacological-management-of-challenging-behaviour-in-learning-disabilities-and-autism.pdf); [Somerset antipsychotic shared-care protocol 2024](https://nhssomerset.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/Antipsychotics-SCP-v3.1-Sept-2024-FINAL.pdf); [Training Matters magazine](https://www.tmmagazine.co.uk/learning-disabilities-part-five)

- A 2025 audit of four UK adult learning-disability teams found that only 40% of patients had a documented medication review strategy and only 29% a documented timescale for stopping. — [Clinical audit, PMC12260889](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12260889/)
- Royal College of Psychiatrists-derived guidance (Challenging Behaviour Foundation): if medication has not had its desired effect within three months, stop it and consider other options. — [CBF Medication Pathway](https://medication.challengingbehaviour.org.uk/pathway/guidance-prescribing-antipsychotic-medication/)

**STOMP (NHS England, 2016)**
- STOMP ("Stopping over medication of people with a learning disability, autism or both") was launched in 2016. A Public Health England estimate says up to 30,000–35,000 adults with a learning disability are prescribed an antipsychotic, an antidepressant or both without appropriate clinical justification. Sources differ on whether this is a daily count or a total of adults. — [NHS England STOMP-STAMP](https://www.england.nhs.uk/learning-disabilities/improving-health/stomp-stamp/); [BPS STOMP](https://bps.org.uk/member-networks/dcp-faculty-people-intellectual-disabilities/STOMP); [Dignity in Care](https://dignityincare.org.uk/Dignity-in-Care-news/STOMP-STopping-the-Over-Medication-of-People-with-a-learning-disability-autism-or-both); [PHE report (gov.uk)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/psychotropic-drugs-and-people-with-learning-disabilities-or-autism/psychotropic-drugs-and-people-with-learning-disabilities-or-autism-executive-summary)

**Prescribing without mental illness: Sheehan et al. 2015 BMJ**
- Sheehan R, Hassiotis A, Walters K, Osborn D, Strydom A, Horsfall L. "Mental illness, challenging behaviour, and psychotropic drug prescribing in people with intellectual disability: UK population based cohort study." BMJ 2015;351:h4326. DOI 10.1136/bmj.h4326. 33,016 adults with ID in UK primary care (THIN, 1999–2013). **71% of those prescribed antipsychotics had no record of severe mental illness.** 26% had neither severe mental illness nor challenging behaviour recorded. Antipsychotic prescribing was associated with challenging behaviour, autism, dementia and older age. Incidence of new psychotropic prescribing was 518 per 10,000 person-years. — [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2015/sep/antipsychotics-inappropriately-prescribed-people-intellectual-disabilities); [Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2015.18281); [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/widespread-prescribing-of-antipsychotics-for-intellectually-disabled-people-revealed)

**Deprescribing evidence**
- Ramerman L, de Kuijper G, Scheers T, Vink M, Vrijmoeth P, Hoekstra PJ. "Is risperidone effective in reducing challenging behaviours in individuals with intellectual disabilities after 1 year or longer use? - The two withdrawal trials conflict (risperidone: no worsening; zuclopenthixol: worsening). This supports individual, slow, monitored taper attempts rather than blanket rules.

## 7. Monitoring recommendations (ECG, weight/BMI/waist, HbA1c/glucose, lipids, prolactin, liver values, EPS, behaviour scales): what interval

### Takeaway
There is no single ID-specific schedule. The usual UK standard (NICE CG178 adults, Maudsley) is: baseline weight, BMI, waist, BP, pulse, FBC, U&E, LFT, fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids, prolactin and ECG, plus EPS assessment. Weight is checked weekly for 6 weeks, then at 12 weeks. Glucose/HbA1c and lipids at 3 months, then annually. Prolactin at 6 months, then annually. ECG after dose changes and ideally annually. NICE's paediatric schedule (CG155) is stricter (12 weeks, then every 6 months), which is often applied in ID. Behaviour should be tracked with a validated scale: the ABC (used in Gagiano and Ramerman) or the MOAS (used in Häßler).

### Cited Findings
- NICE adult antipsychotic monitoring (CG178, as summarised by NHS Scotland Right Decisions): prolactin 6 months after starting, then every 12 months; lipids 3 months after starting, then every 12 months; plasma glucose or HbA1c 3 months after starting, then every 12 months; ECG after dose changes and ideally annually. — [NHS Scotland Right Decisions: NICE antipsychotic monitoring](https://rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/media/3hgfujah/nice-guidelines-antipsychotic-monitoring.pdf)
- Maudsley-based audit standard, baseline: FBC, HbA1c, U&E, fasting lipids, fasting glucose, LFTs, weight and BP. — [RCPsych "101 Recipes for Audit in Psychiatry": atypical antipsychotics monitoring](https://cambridge.org/core/books/101-recipes-for-audit-in-psychiatry/atypical-antipsychotics-monitoring/021F57D45336A7692331BB7301503116)
- NICE CG155 (children and young people): baseline investigations, weekly monitoring for the first 6 weeks, at 12 weeks, then every 6 months (weight, height, waist/hip, pulse, BP, fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids, prolactin, movement disorders, nutrition). — [NICE CG155 supplementary information](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/cg155/chapter/supplementary-information-on-baseline-investigations-and-monitoring)
- Learning-disability service QI work states that everyone on antipsychotics should have baseline and annual monitoring, including weight, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile, BP, pulse and movement-disorder assessment. — [ELFT LD QI paper (BMJ Quality Improvement Reports)](https://qi.elft.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/improving-physical-health-for-people-taking-antipsychotic-medication-in-the-community-LDS.pdf)
- A UK NHS trust psychotropic monitoring guide gives local schedules per drug. — [TEWV Psychotropic monitoring guide](https://tewv.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Psychotropic-monitoring-guide.pdf)
- Behaviour rating scales used in the pivotal ID trials: **ABC** (Gagiano 2005; Ramerman 2019) and **MOAS** (Häßler 2007). — [Huddersfield eprint (Gagiano)](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/668/); [Zendy (Ramerman)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/jir.12584); [Cambridge PDF (Häßler)](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/88773ABF6A182A7F579BBDE108E1827A/S0007125000172204a.pdf/zuclopenthixol_in_adults_with_intellectual_disabilities_and_aggressive_behaviours.pdf)

### Inferences
- For this patient (already obese, constipated), the following would be prudent: baseline plus monthly weight and waist in the first 3 months; HbA1c and lipids at baseline, 3 months, then every 6–12 months; prolactin at baseline and 3–6 months (risperidone, or if gynaecomastia or sexual dysfunction appears); ECG at baseline and after each dose increase (especially melperone, pipamperone, zuclopenthixol, any combination, or with CYP2D6 inhibition); bowel diary (constipation can itself trigger agitation in nonverbal people); EPS and akathisia observation (akathisia may look like more restlessness or aggression in a nonverbal patient); and an ABC or MOAS baseline before starting, repeated at 3–4 and 6 weeks.
- Lithium (if used) needs serum levels, TSH, creatinine/eGFR and calcium. Valproate needs LFT, FBC and ammonia if clinically indicated (standard practice; not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No German-language ID-specific monitoring standard was found.
- Lithium and valproate monitoring intervals were not sourced.
- No published validation was found for the ABC or MOAS in Angelman syndrome specifically.

## 8. Metformin co-prescription to prevent or treat antipsychotic-induced weight gain

### Takeaway
Metformin reduces antipsychotic-associated weight gain by about 3 kg on average vs placebo in schizophrenia RCTs. In autistic youth on atypical antipsychotics, it reduced weight by about 2.7 kg over 16 weeks and was well tolerated without increasing irritability. No ID-adult RCT was found. In Germany this would be off-label.

### Cited Findings
- de Silva VA, Suraweera C, Ratnatunga SS, Dayabandara M, Wanniarachchi N, Hanwella R. "Metformin in prevention and treatment of antipsychotic induced weight gain: a systematic review and meta-analysis." BMC Psychiatry 2016;16(1):341. 12 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs, n=743 (schizophrenia/schizoaffective). **Weight −3.27 kg (95% CI −4.66 to −1.89)**, BMI −1.13 kg/m², improved insulin resistance, and no significant change in fasting glucose. Metformin was not then included in guidelines for this indication. — [PubMed 27716110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27716110/); [Springer full text PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12888-016-1049-5.pdf)
- Anagnostou E, et al. "Metformin for treatment of overweight induced by atypical antipsychotic medication in young people with autism spectrum disorder: a randomized clinical trial." JAMA Psychiatry 2016 (online 24 Aug 2016). 61 randomised, aged 6–17, on stable atypical antipsychotics with weight gain, 16 weeks, 4 sites. BMI z-score difference −0.10 (95% CI −0.16 to −0.04; p=.003). **Raw weight −2.73 kg (95% CI −4.04 to −1.43)**, BMI −0.95. Well tolerated, with no increase in irritability. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/634946); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20160823/metformin-effective-for-weight-gain-associated-with-atypical-antipsychotic-use)

### Inferences
- If an antipsychotic is started in this already-obese patient, consider metformin at the start (preventive) or when weight gain of ≥5–7% appears. That threshold is common practice and not sourced here. Use would be off-label (metformin is approved for type 2 diabetes).
- GI side effects of metformin (diarrhoea) might paradoxically help a constipation tendency but could cause discomfort-related agitation in a nonverbal person (inference).

### Gaps
- No RCT of metformin in adults with ID was found.
- Newer evidence (GLP-1 receptor agonists for antipsychotic weight gain, 2020s) was not searched.
- No current (2024–2026) guideline recommending metformin co-prescription was verified.

## 9. Angelman-specific context relevant to drug choice (UPD genotype, obesity and hyperphagia, anxiety, aggression, sleep, constipation)

### Takeaway
UPD-type Angelman syndrome typically has a milder phenotype, **less epilepsy (about 20%)** and a **higher obesity risk** than deletion type. Aggression is common and may increase in adulthood. It is often linked to anxiety (separation) and social motivation rather than directly to UBE3A loss. Constipation, sleep problems and overweight are frequent in adults. Behaviour-based interventions are considered the standard of care. Psychotropic evidence in AS is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- UPD has a milder phenotype, higher obesity risk, less epilepsy (about 20%) and better motor skills. — [ERN-ITHACA webinar "Angelman syndrome in clinical practice", May 2025 (PDF)](https://ern-ithaca.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-05-13-Webinar_Angelman-syndrome-in-clinical-practice_Final-Version.pdf)
- In non-deletion AS, UPD patients have milder symptoms and higher BMI. BMI, head circumference and seizure activity differ most between molecular classes. — [Non-deletion AS genotype study (PMC4802915)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4802915)
- Hyperphagia: one account reports more hyperphagia in UPD children than in deletion cases. Another study found elevated hyperphagia scores without genotype association, so the evidence conflicts. — [FIND resources: Feeding difficulties in AS](https://findresources.co.uk/the-syndromes/angelman/behaviourfood); [search summary of genotype studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6350130)
- In a caregiver-reported study of 110 adolescents and adults with AS, 32% were overweight or obese (more often women). — [search summary; see "Angelman syndrome in adulthood" reference](https://insight.jci.org/references/scholar/4097/B47)
- Wheeler AC, et al. "Unmet clinical needs and burden in Angelman syndrome: a review of the literature." Orphanet J Rare Dis 2017 (PMC5644259). Physical aggression is reported in up to 73% of adolescents with AS and may increase in adulthood. Behaviour-based interventions (e.g. ABA, discrimination training) are the standard of care. Antipsychotics (risperidone, aripiprazole, olanzapine) are listed among the options used. — [PMC5644259](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5644259/); [OJRD table](https://ojrd.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13023-017-0716-z/tables/3)
- Angelman Syndrome Foundation: reported prevalence of challenging behaviour such as aggression ranges from <10% to 100%, and there is no evidence of a direct link between UBE3A deficiency and aggressive behaviour. — [ASF Behavior Training Series](https://angelman.org/resources/behavior-training-series/)
- Duis J, et al. "A multidisciplinary approach and consensus statement to establish standards of care for Angelman syndrome." Mol Genet Genomic Med 2022 (PMC8922964), with input from more than 20 key opinion leaders. Its behaviour and psychopharmacology content could **not** be retrieved. — [PMC8922964](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8922964/); [ASF standards-of-care PDF (2025)](https://angelman.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/standards-of-care-for-angelman-syndrome.pdf)
- Dutch adult AS cohort (den Besten et al. 2021, "Clinical aspects of a large group of adults with Angelman syndrome"): constipation, reflux, visual problems, scoliosis, behavioural and sleeping problems were frequent. — [PMC7756639](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7756639/)
- Spanish AS cohort (30 patients, median age 22.7): after epilepsy, the most frequent comorbidities were psychiatric symptoms, scoliosis, overweight, constipation and ophthalmological problems. — [Revista de Neurología 2023](https://neurologia.com/articulo/2022235/eng)
- A case report describes testosterone-mediated aggression in AS treated with leuprolide and orchiectomy (title-level information only). — [PMC9184178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9184178/)

### Inferences
- Drug choice should weigh obesity and possible hyperphagia (UPD) heavily, which means avoiding appetite-increasing drugs where possible. The lower epilepsy risk in UPD makes seizure-threshold concerns relevant but less dominant than in deletion AS.
- Since attention-maintained and food-denial aggression is learned, the main lever is behavioural: functional communication training with AAC, a structured food plan, and non-contingent attention. Medication can at most lower arousal or impulsivity so that behavioural work becomes possible.
- Constipation and sleep fragmentation are treatable drivers of irritability in nonverbal patients. Optimising them (laxatives, sleep hygiene, melatonin) should come before or alongside psychotropics. Anticholinergic or sedating drugs may worsen constipation.

### Gaps
- No Angelman-specific RCTs of psychotropics for aggression were found.
- The Duis 2022 consensus behaviour/psychiatry recommendations could not be retrieved (NCBI and angelman.org blocked).
- No adult UPD-specific behaviour or obesity data were found.

## 10. German regulatory and practical aspects: off-label use, legal guardian consent, freiheitsentziehende Maßnahmen (§ 1831 BGB)

### Takeaway
Most pharmacological options for aggression in an adult with ID are off-label in Germany. Exceptions are zuclopenthixol (labelled for agitation in ID) and melperone/pipamperone (labelled for non-specific agitation). Off-label use requires a careful benefit-risk case, documented informed consent (by the legal guardian if appointed for health care) and possibly a statutory health insurance (GKV) reimbursement check. Under § 1831 Abs. 4 BGB (Betreuungsrecht since 2023), **medication used to deprive a resident of liberty in an institution requires court approval** (Betreuungsgericht). That can apply to regular sedating medication in a group home.

### Cited Findings
- § 1831 Abs. 4 BGB: liberty can be deprived "durch mechanische Vorrichtungen, Medikamente oder auf andere Weise" for a person in a hospital, home or other institution. This requires approval by the Betreuungsgericht, and the guardian needs the appropriate area of responsibility. Grounds under Abs. 1 centre on self-endangerment. — [§ 1831 BGB (gesetze-im-internet.de)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__1831.html); [betanet: freiheitsentziehende Maßnahmen](https://betanet.de/pdf/687)
- Sedating medication prescribed over weeks counts as a freiheitsentziehende Maßnahme. **Whether drugs that only reduce the urge to move as a side effect are covered is disputed.** In acute danger, measures may be taken without prior approval, but the court must be informed and approval sought afterwards. The court decision specifies which measure is allowed for what maximum period. — [betanet](https://betanet.de/pdf/687); [AG Passau Merkblatt freiheitsentziehende Maßnahmen](https://www.justiz.bayern.de/media/images/behoerden-und-gerichte/amtsgerichte/passau/merkblatt_freiheitsentziehende_maßnahmen.pdf)
- Approval is usually preceded by an expert opinion or at least a medical certificate. — [Lexikon Betreuungsrecht (search summary)](https://www.lexikon-betreuungsrecht.de/Spezial:Mobiler_Unterschied/61099...65370)
- Off-label use of psychotropics is frequent in German ID care. — [Warnke, PPT 2010](https://www.ppt-online.de/_Resources/Persistent/e/7/5/1/e75131fbacd7884189e3d720b73411aa2d07025c/ppt201003_artikel601.pdf)
- Risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine and aripiprazole are the most frequently off-label-prescribed atypical antipsychotics. — [Off-label use of atypical antipsychotics review (PMC10434096)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10434096/)

### Inferences
- In a group home (an "Einrichtung"), a regular sedating antipsychotic or benzodiazepine given mainly to calm the person or keep them from moving about could trigger § 1831 Abs. 4. Prescribers, guardians and the institution should check whether court approval is needed. Treatment aimed at an underlying condition (therapeutic intent) is generally viewed differently. Legal review is advisable.
- Aggression *towards others* is not a ground for civil-law (Betreuungsrecht) deprivation of liberty, which centres on self-endangerment. Danger to others falls under state mental health laws (PsychKG/PsychKHG). This is an inference to be verified by a legal source.

### Gaps
- No sourced German information was found on GKV reimbursement rules for off-label prescribing (BSG criteria; § 35c SGB V; G-BA Anlage VI). Under background knowledge (**unverified**), off-label prescriptions may be refused by the Krankenkasse unless BSG criteria are met (serious illness, no alternative, reasonable prospect of success). Many are reimbursed in practice. Verify.
- No sourced German guidance on informed-consent documentation for off-label psychotropics in ID (e.g. § 630e BGB). Also no info on the 2023 Betreuungsrecht reform's effect on medical consent (§ 1827ff BGB).
- Not verified whether BGH case law explicitly classifies a particular antipsychotic regimen in a group home as a freiheitsentziehende Maßnahme.
