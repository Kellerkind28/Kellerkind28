## 1. Evidence that function-based behavioural interventions reduce aggression/challenging behaviour in AS (designs, n, effects)

### Takeaway
The AS-specific evidence consists entirely of small **single-case experimental studies in children** (n = 3–12 per study, < 30 children in total). No studies in adults and no RCTs were found. These studies consistently show three things:
1. Challenging behaviour in AS has identifiable, *individually varying* functions: attention, tangibles/food, escape, or social-interaction-related.
2. Functional Communication Training (FCT) and discrimination training reduce it, with small-to-large effects.
3. Effects require sustained, systematic teaching.

The AS behavioural phenotype has two features that make these function-based approaches particularly plausible for this patient: a strong social motivation and elevated aggression.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence/phenotype (Obs):**
- **Arron, Oliver, Moss, Berg & Burbidge (2011).** "The prevalence and phenomenology of self-injurious and aggressive behaviour in genetic syndromes", *J Intellect Disabil Res* 55(2):109–120 (DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2788.2010.01337.x [TK]).

  - **Aggression was elevated in AS** (and in Smith-Magenis syndrome), whereas self-injury was *not* elevated in AS — [Birmingham Research Portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/the-prevalence-and-phenomenology-of-self-injurious-and-aggressive/); [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/the-prevalence-and-phenomenology-of-self-injurious-and-aggressive-beha/)
- **Powis & Oliver (2014)**, review "The prevalence of aggression in genetic syndromes". - **Summers et al. (1995)**, "Behaviour problems in Angelman syndrome". Reported problems included hyperactivity, short attention span, eating and sleeping problems, aggression and noncompliance (Obs) — [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/behaviour-problems-in-angelman-syndrome/)

**Social motivation phenotype — relevant to "attention-maintained" aggression towards mother, grandmother and selected carers:**
- **Oliver, Horsler, Berg, Bellamy, Dick & Griffiths (2007).** "Genomic imprinting and the expression of affect in Angelman syndrome: what's in the smile?", *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 48(6):571–579 [TK for volume/pages]. Experimental observation (Obs/experimental):
  - Children with AS smiled more than a comparison group in all settings, and more when adult attention was high.
  - Their smiling evoked more adult attention, eye contact and smiling.
  - The authors conclude that smiling *accesses social resources* — [PDF via FIND resources](https://findresources.co.uk/uploads/Angelman%20Videos/Research%20articles/Oliver_et_al_2007_Smiling_in_AS_JCPP.pdf); [Portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/genomic-imprinting-and-the-expression-of-affect-in-angelman-syndr)

The study compared responses to mothers vs strangers while manipulating eye contact and speech — [Portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-adult-familiarity-on-social-behaviours-in-angelman-syn); [OUCI](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4EAprRB4)
- **Adams, Horsler & Oliver (2011).**
  - Design: n = 24 children; conditions were proximity-only, restricted social interaction and full social interaction.
  - Finding: smiling and laughing declined in the oldest group (13.4–15.9 y) only in the social-interaction condition, i.e. age-related change in social behaviour — [PDF](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/Documents/college-les/psych/cerebra/Age-related-change-in-social-behavior-in-children-with-Angelman-syndrome.pdf)
- **Horsler & Oliver (2006).** "The behavioural phenotype of Angelman syndrome", *JIDR* (review). **Functional analysis (FA) and function-based intervention studies in AS (all SCED, children):**
- **Strachan, Shaw, Burrow, Horsler, Allen & Oliver (2009).** "Experimental functional analysis of aggression in children with Angelman syndrome", *Res Dev Disabil* 30(5):1095–1106 [TK for vol/pages; DOI 10.1016/j.ridd.2009.03.005 TK].
  - Design: n = 12 children, analogue experimental FA.
  - The authors hypothesised attention-maintained aggression, and this was **mostly not supported**:
    - only **1/12** showed an attention-maintained pattern;
    - **3/12** showed more aggression *during* social interaction;
    - **2/12** showed escape-maintained aggression.
  - Limitations: low numbers of aggressive episodes and young age — [Portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/experimental-functional-analysis-of-aggression-in-children-with-a/); [PDF](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/2922816/Strachan_et_al._(2009).pdf); [summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/experimental-functional-analysis-of-aggression-in-children-with-angelm/)
- **Radstaake, Didden, Oliver, Allen & Curfs (2012).** "Functional analysis and functional communication training in individuals with Angelman syndrome", *Developmental Neurorehabilitation* 15(2):91–104, DOI [10.3109/17518423.2011.651537](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.3109/17518423.2011.651537). Note that this paper is often cited as 2013 but was published 2012.
  - Design: n = 4 children, FA followed by FCT with **precursor-based prompting**, i.e. prompting the communicative response as soon as precursor behaviours appear.
  - Functions identified: attention, tangibles and escape.
  - FCT reduced challenging behaviour when precursors were used as the onset of prompting, with **effects "ranging from small to large"** (SCED) — [Birmingham Portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/functional-analysis-and-functional-communication-training-in-indi/)
- **Radstaake, Didden, Lang, O'Reilly et al. (classroom study).** DOI 10.1007/s10882-012-9302-4. Journal and year: *J Dev Phys Disabil* 2013;25:49–63 [TK, not verified].
  - Design: n = 3 children; FA and FCT protocols administered **in the classroom by their own teachers**.
  - Outcome: in all three children challenging behaviour decreased as a function of FCT (SCED).
  - The study is also a chapter in Radstaake's 2015 dissertation — [ifp.nyu.edu abstract](https://ifp.nyu.edu/?p=397111)
- **Heald, Allen, Villa & Oliver (2013).** "Discrimination training reduces high rate social approach behaviors in Angelman syndrome: proof of principle", *Res Dev Disabil* 34(5):1794–1803, PMID 23518390.
  - Design: n = 4 children; multiple-schedule arrangement in which social reinforcement and extinction alternated, each signalled by a novel cue stimulus.
  - Results:
    - All four learned the discrimination.
    - Approach rates fell in the extinction-signalled condition, but only after **16–20 teaching sessions**.
    - Reversal effects occurred in 2 children.
  - Authors' conclusion: the cue can be part of a broader intervention, but it needs sustained teaching (SCED) — [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/23518390/); [SSBP poster](https://findresources.co.uk/uploads/Angelman%20Videos/Posters/SSBP%20poster_Mary%20Heald.pdf); [Birmingham record](https://birmingham.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/discrimination-training-reduces-high-rate-social-approach-behavio/fingerprints/)
- **Fichtner & Tiger (2015).** "Teaching discriminated social approaches to individuals with Angelman syndrome", *J Appl Behav Anal* 48(4), DOI 10.1002/jaba.237.
  - Design: n = 3 boys with frequent hugging/hand-holding.
  - Outcome: discriminated social approaches were taught, then replicated across caregivers and extended into homes (SCED) — [zendy/DOI](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jaba.237); [MINDS@UW](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/eb0230e5-ac32-4643-9471-4787b4169f8e)
- **Summers (2012).** "Neurodevelopmental outcomes in children with Angelman syndrome after 1 year of behavioural intervention", *Dev Neurorehabil* 15:239–252, DOI 10.3109/17518423.2012.676101. The citation is confirmed; the findings could not be retrieved — [reference list](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.3109/17518423.2011.651537)
- **2012 Expert Panel on Challenging Behaviours in AS.** Funded by the Canadian Angelman Syndrome Society and the Angelman Syndrome Foundation (ASF), and chaired by Dr. Jane Summers, it aimed to produce guidelines for understanding and addressing challenging behaviour (Cons) — [ASF collaborative research](https://www.angelman.org/collaborative-research)
- **Summers, book chapter on Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) for challenging behaviour in AS**, in Calculator (ed.) *Angelman Syndrome: Communication, Educational and Related Considerations* (Bentham). It focuses on **prevention and FCT** (Cons/expert) — [EurekaSelect chapter](https://www.eurekaselect.com/chapter/8187)

**General ID/autism evidence on FCT and extinction (meta-analyses of SCED):**
- **Chezan, Wolfe & Drasgow (2018)**, meta-analysis of FCT using Tau-U. FCT reduced problem behaviour and increased communicative responses, with large effect sizes:
  - **ID ES = 0.72**
  - autism ES = 0.64
  - developmental delay ES = 0.67
  - [ASHA Evidence Map](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/e00ad70f-a869-49fe-ae08-c169338d59b1)
- **Heath et al. (2015)**, meta-analysis of FCT across communication mode, age and disability:
  - large effect in autism (Robust IRD 0.79);
  - **moderate effects in ID**;
  - **aided AAC produced larger effects than unaided** (IRD 0.74 vs 0.48).
  - [ASHA Evidence Map](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/076ba697-b5f4-494e-98a8-e48e74e31c61)
- **Lerman & Iwata (1995).** "Prevalence of the extinction burst and its attenuation during treatment", *J Appl Behav Anal* 28(1):93–94, DOI 10.1901/jaba.1995.28-93.
  - Design: 113 extinction data sets.
  - Bursting occurred in 24% overall.
  - **Extinction alone produced bursting in 36%, vs 12% when extinction was combined with other procedures** such as FCT or differential reinforcement (Obs/record review) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16795857/); [DOI page](https://zendy.io/title/10.1901/jaba.1995.28-93)

### Inferences
- The family describes the aggression as attention-maintained, triggered when specific persons withhold attention. This is plausible given the AS social-motivation phenotype (Oliver 2007; Mount 2011). However, Strachan 2009 found pure attention-maintenance in only 1/12 children, and Radstaake 2012 found mixed functions. A formal **functional behaviour assessment (FBA)** in the adult's real settings is therefore mandatory before any plan. The "food denied" and "must sit still/comply" triggers suggest **additional tangible (food) and escape/demand functions**.
- Practical implication of Lerman & Iwata 1995: simply "ignoring" attention-maintained aggression (planned ignoring = extinction) in a strong 82 kg adult risks a dangerous **extinction burst**. Extinction should only be used:
  - combined with FCT or differential reinforcement (DRA, DRO);
  - with a safety plan;
  - consistently across all carers, including mother and grandmother.
  Inconsistent intermittent reinforcement (sometimes giving in) strengthens the behaviour [TK, standard behavioural principle].
- Heald 2013 and Fichtner & Tiger 2015 directly model a usable strategy: a **visual cue signalling when attention is available vs not available**, e.g. a coloured card or wristband worn by the carer. This needs many sessions and must be generalised across persons.
- Overall evidence level for AS-specific behavioural intervention: **low-to-moderate (multiple SCEDs, consistent direction, children only)**. It is extrapolated to adults through the broader ID literature, where FCT shows large effects in SCED meta-analyses.

## 2. What do NICE NG11/NG93, the 2022 AS consensus (Duis et al.) and the broader ID evidence say about behavioural interventions being first-line and medication being second-line?

### Takeaway
- **NICE NG11 (2015)** positions functional assessment and psychological/behavioural interventions as first-line. Antipsychotics are to be *considered only* when such interventions have failed within an agreed time, when treating coexisting conditions has not helped, or when risk is very severe — and then only *in combination with* psychological interventions, with planned review and discontinuation.
- **Duis et al. 2022** lists functional behavioural assessment and ABA/behaviour therapy among recommended multidisciplinary services for AS. It explicitly acknowledges that AS care rests largely on expert experience because evidence is limited.
- RCT evidence in ID adults shows that **antipsychotics did not outperform placebo for aggression** (Tyrer 2008).
- Staff-training-only Positive Behaviour Support (PBS) as implemented in one UK trial **did not reduce challenging behaviour** (Hassiotis 2018). Implementation quality matters.

### Cited Findings
- **NICE NG11** "Challenging behaviour and learning disabilities: prevention and interventions for people with learning disabilities whose behaviour challenges" (2015). It covers children, young people and adults, and support for families and carers (Cons) — [NICE NG11](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11); [full guideline (NCCMH)](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11/evidence/full-guideline-2311243668); [NICE "do not do" list](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11/resources/do-not-do)
- **NG11, antipsychotic condition (secondary sources).** "The use of antipsychotic medication to manage behaviour that challenges should only be considered if psychological or other interventions alone do not produce change within an agreed time **and** the risk to the person or others is very severe" — [National Health Executive](https://www.nationalhealthexecutive.com/News/medication-should-not-be-first-resort-to-challenging-behaviour--nice-/108011). The Challenging Behaviour Foundation medication pathway gives "only… if psychological or other interventions alone do not reduce the challenging behaviour within an agreed time" as one of several criteria — [CBF Medication Pathway](https://medication.challengingbehaviour.org.uk/pathway/guidance-prescribing-antipsychotic-medication/). **The two secondary sources conflict** on whether the criteria are cumulative ("and") or alternative ("or").
- **[TK] NG11 wording as remembered (rec. 1.9.x):**
  - "Consider antipsychotic medication to manage behaviour that challenges only if: psychological or other interventions alone do not produce change within an agreed time; **or** treatment for any coexisting mental or physical health problem has not led to a reduction in the behaviour; **or** the risk to the person or others is very severe (for example, because of violence, aggression or self-injury)."
  - "Only offer antipsychotic medication **in combination with** psychological or other interventions."
  - Start low, review effectiveness and side effects after 3–4 weeks, and **stop if there is no indication of response at 6 weeks**.

- Germany has no dedicated STOMP-style national programme visible in search results. The closest German equivalents are:
  - the Ulmer Maßnahmenkatalog;
  - the DGSGB/WPA guideline translation;
  - the AWMF S2k guideline;
  - for the legal side, the court-approval requirement of § 1831 Abs. 4 BGB (see section 6).

## 3. Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC / Unterstützte Kommunikation, UK) in AS — does it reduce challenging behaviour?

### Takeaway
- AAC is an established, recommended approach for improving communication in AS.
- The direct evidence that AAC *by itself* reduces challenging behaviour in AS is **weak or absent**. The link is mainly indirect, via FCT, where aided AAC as the replacement response yields larger effects than unaided modes.
- One 2024 dissertation even found a *positive* association between communication ability and challenging behaviour in AS, with no moderating effect of AAC use.

### Cited Findings
- **Roche, Sigafoos & Trembath (2020).** "Augmentative and Alternative Communication Intervention for People With Angelman Syndrome: a Systematic Review" (systematic review of 9 studies).
  - Conclusion: AAC intervention is "a viable approach for enhancing the communication skills" of people with AS, and larger studies are needed.
  - Outcomes were communication skills, *not* behaviour — [Hopkins Centre](https://www.hopkinscentre.edu.au/publication/augmentative-and-alternative-communication-550)
- **Feck C (2024)**, LSU PhD dissertation (Obs).
  - Found a **positive relationship between communication ability and challenging behaviour** in AS, contrary to expectations from other populations.
  - **AAC use did not moderate** this relationship.
  - The author calls for social/communicative intervention research — [LSU Repository](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_dissertations/6504)
- **ASF "Aggression as Communicative Behavior"**, Behavior Series. It frames aggression in AS as communication and AAC/FCT as replacement behaviour (Cons/educational) — [ASF PDF](https://angelman.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Aggression-as-Communicative-Behavior.pdf); [ASF Behavior Series page](https://www.angelman.org/aggression-as-communication/)
- **Calculator SN (ed.).** *Angelman Syndrome: Communication, Educational and Related Considerations* (Bentham Books). It includes:
  - chapters on AAC;
  - a chapter by Iacono & Johnson on AAC for adults with AS in post-school, home and community settings;
  - Summers' ABA chapter.
  (Cons/expert) — [Bentham book page](https://benthambooks.com/book/9781681081168/foreword/); [EurekaSelect chapters](https://www.eurekaselect.com/chapter/8189)

- **German-language resource:** Waldschmidt F (2017), *Unterstützte Kommunikation bei Schülern mit Angelman-Syndrom* (school-age focus). It notes that communication impairment accounts for a large part of the disability in AS — [listing](https://www.ebay.de/p/13042162708). ### Inferences
- The patient is nonverbal but understands speech very well. This profile fits **aided AAC** best: a communication book, a speech-generating device or app, or pictorial "I want attention/I want X/pause" buttons. Within an FCT framework, AAC is the replacement behaviour for "give me attention", "I want sweets" and "I need a break". Teaching a *break request* and an *attention request* is directly linked to his aggression triggers.
- AAC should be framed as a necessary condition (giving him a non-aggressive way to achieve the same function), not as a stand-alone behaviour treatment. The Feck 2024 finding is a caution against expecting that AAC alone reduces aggression.
- **Visual schedules/predictability** (time timer, first-then boards, visual daily plan): the general ID/autism behavioural literature supports these as antecedent strategies [TK]. In this session no AS-specific study was found.

## 4. Medical/physical causes of aggression to rule out in nonverbal adults with AS (incl. tools and diagnostic overshadowing)

### Takeaway
GI problems are extremely common in AS:
- constipation in 71–81%;
- gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD) in 44–53%;
- sleep problems in up to 72% of adults.
Visual problems, scoliosis and obesity are frequent in adults. For a nonverbal man with *hard stools*, BMI ~30 and "avoids exertion", the medical work-up before (or in parallel with) any psychotropic should include:
- constipation;
- GERD/oesophagitis;
- dental pain;
- ENT/otitis;
- musculoskeletal pain (scoliosis, hips);
- vision;
- sleep disorders, including obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA, a plausible risk given his obesity);
- hunger/food-related issues.
Distress tools (DisDAT) help to detect changes from his individual baseline.

### Cited Findings
- **Glassman LW, et al. (2017).** "Prevalence of gastrointestinal symptoms in Angelman syndrome", *Am J Med Genet A* 173(10):2703–2709, DOI 10.1002/ajmg.a.38401, PMID 28816003 (Obs, retrospective).

### Inferences
- **"Fairly hard stools"** in a man with AS, given 71–81% constipation prevalence, is a priority finding. Untreated constipation/abdominal pain is a classic, treatable driver of aggression in nonverbal adults. A structured bowel regimen should come **before** psychotropics, because several antipsychotics are themselves constipating and weight-increasing [TK].
- Further work-up items [TK — clinical reasoning, not source-verified in this session]:

## 5. Psychiatric differential diagnosis in adults with ID/AS and the emotional-development approach (SEED)

### Takeaway
- **Anxiety, especially separation-related distress from a preferred caregiver, is common in AS.** It rises with age and is statistically linked with aggression and sleep problems.
- Before attributing aggression purely to "learned strategy", the assessment should consider anxiety/separation distress, depression, ADHD (hyperactivity is part of the AS phenotype and tends to decrease with age) and pain.
- Adapted diagnostic frameworks exist:
  - **DM-ID-2** (NADD) adapts DSM-5 criteria, with a separate section for severe/profound ID.
  - **SEED/SEED-2** (Sappok et al.) estimates the *emotional reference age* across 8 domains, used to tailor expectations and environment.

### Cited Findings
- **Wheeler AC, et al. (2019, *J Intellect Disabil Res*)**, anxiety/separation-related behaviours in AS (Obs, n = 100). Title per [TK]: "Anxiety-associated and separation distress-associated behaviours in Angelman syndrome".
  - **Anxiety concerns in 40%**.
  - **~70% had a preferred caregiver, and over half showed distress when separated from that caregiver**.
  - Aggression and sleep difficulties were consistent predictors of total anxiety; the authors call for disentangling anxiety from aggression/anger.
  - Participants with the deletion genotype were **less** likely to have anxiety behaviours.
  - [PDF via ERN-ITHACA](https://ern-ithaca.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Wheeler_Angelman_behaviour_JIDR2019.pdf)
- **Wheeler AC, Sacco P, Cabo R (2017)**, "Unmet clinical needs and burden in Angelman syndrome: a review of the literature" (*Orphanet J Rare Dis*) (review). Some issues improve with age, such as hyperactivity, while **others worsen, such as movement disorders, aggression and anxiety** — [SpringerMedizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/unmet-clinical-needs-and-burden-in-angelman-syndrome-a-review-of/15138620)
- **Prasad et al. (2018):** anxiety increases significantly with age; defiant behaviours decrease — [CAS record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,29696750)
- **Genotype:** UPD is generally associated with a milder phenotype than deletion. Deletion is the most severe across domains — [search summary of reviews, PMC6732290/PMC5644259](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6732290)
- **ASF:** people with AS are at risk of psychiatric illness like anyone else — [ASF Behavior Series](https://angelman.org/mental-health-aggression)
- **Anxiety treatment (patient-education level):** SSRIs are listed alongside routines and other therapies for anxiety in AS (low-level source, no evidence grading) — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/anxiety-in-angelman-syndrome/)
- **DM-ID-2** (*Diagnostic Manual – Intellectual Disability*, 2nd ed.; NADD; Fletcher, Barnhill, McCarthy & Strydom, 2016/2017).
  - It adapts DSM-5 for people with ID, with separate sections for mild–moderate ID, **severe/profound ID** and children.
  - Modifications include **symptom equivalents** and changed duration/age requirements.
  - NADD released an addendum aligning it with DSM-5-TR (Cons) — [NADD DM-ID page](https://thenadd.org/?p=600); [NADD addendum announcement](https://thenadd.org/?p=30375); [clinical usefulness paper](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/11751_clinical-usefulness-diagnostic-manual-intellectual.pdf)
- **SEED** (*Skala der Emotionalen Entwicklung – Diagnostik*; Sappok et al.).
  - Purpose: a standardised instrument based on the emotional-development approach, for **differential-diagnostic classification of behavioural disorders and challenging behaviour** in people with intellectual developmental disorder. Hogrefe states that it helps align care with the person's basic emotional needs.
  - **Original SEED:** 200 items; emotional reference age 0–12 years; 8 domains, from relating to one's own body and to caregivers through communication and affect regulation; α = .99 and inter-rater κ = 1.0 reported.
  - **SEED-2 (2023):** 6 phases per domain, corresponding to emotional reference ages 0–18 years; 5 yes/no items per phase.
    - Cronbach's α .94 (118 children with ID) and .92 (83 adults without mental illness).
    - For use in disability services and psychiatric practices/clinics by psychologists, (special) educators and psychiatrists.
    - Purchase requires proof of qualification.
    - The English SED-S has one phase fewer.
  - (Instrument/validation) — [Hogrefe SEED-2](https://www.hogrefe.com/de/shop/skala-der-emotionalen-entwicklung-diagnostik-2-99468.html); [EU-SELF profile](https://euself.nbu.bg/media/001/generated/AM-064-SEED.pdf); [Hogrefe material collection](https://www.hogrefe.com/de/shop/eine-materialsammlung-zur-praktischen-anwendung-der-seed-skala-der-emotionalen-entwicklung-diagnostik.html)
- **Neumann, Calabrese & Sappok (Psychiatr Prax, 2025, DOI 10.1055/a-2546-3131)**, qualitative item analysis of SEED-2. The greatest need for revision was the separability of some domains and **phases 4 and 5** (ages 8–12) — [Thieme](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/a-2546-3131)
- **Practice recommendation:** the Psychotherapeutenkammer Berlin workshop report names SEED as a diagnostic instrument for people with ID. It recommends Sappok & Zepperitz, *Das Alter der Gefühle* (2019), as an introduction — [PTK Berlin report](https://psychotherapeutenkammer-berlin.de/system/files/document/Bericht_Workshop%202.pdf)
- **Evaluation of a training course** on the emotional-development approach for carers of people with ID (Switzerland, 2020) — [Rosenfluh PDF](https://www.rosenfluh.ch/media/psychiatrie-neurologie/2020/04/Emotionale-Entwicklung-besser-verstehen-Evaluation-eines-Weiterbildungskurses-in-der-Begleitung-von-Menschen-mit-Intelligenzminderung.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Emotional-development reading [TK — based on the Došen/Sappok model; phase labels and ages not verified in this session]:**
  - The family reports a play level of ~3–4 years.
  - If a SEED-2 profile showed a reference age around **SEO 3 ("erste Individuation", ~18–36 months)** or the transition to **SEO 4 ("Identifikation", ~3–7 years)**, the predicted needs would be:
    - secure attachment and the physical availability of familiar persons;
    - autonomy-seeking and "Nein"/testing limits;
    - a very limited ability to wait, delay gratification or regulate frustration.
  - In that case **"sich unterordnen", sitting still and complying with demands are developmentally over-demanding expectations**. Escape-maintained aggression in those situations is then predictable rather than "bad behaviour".
  - Environmental design would follow:
    - short demand units with built-in movement;
    - choices instead of commands;
    - predictable routines and visual schedules;
    - a stable small team of key persons (Bezugspersonen);
    - planned "attention windows";
    - avoiding power struggles over food;
    - a low-arousal approach.
- The **preferred-caregiver / separation-distress profile** in Wheeler 2019 (70%/>50%) fits the reported aggression when mother/grandmother/selected carers do not attend to him. This raises the possibility that part of the "attention-seeking" is **anxiety/attachment-driven**. That has implications both for:
  - behavioural planning: e.g. transition rituals, predictable contact schedules with family, and a gradual build-up of new Bezugspersonen in the group home;
  - pharmacology: if an anxiety disorder is diagnosed, the target would be anxiety (e.g. SSRI) rather than sedation.
  This needs psychiatric assessment with DM-ID-2/SEED.
- The ADHD-like signs need to be weighed against several alternatives:
  - the AS hyperactivity phenotype, which usually decreases with age;
  - sleep deprivation/OSA;
  - under-stimulation, given that he "craves experiences" — this suggests an **activity- and experience-rich daily structure** as an intervention;
  - anxiety.

- No published study using SEED specifically in AS was found.

## 6. German legal and ethical framework: § 1831 BGB (incl. sedating medication in an Einrichtung), § 1832 BGB, Betreuer consent, case law, Werdenfelser Weg, UN-CRPD — what must family/Betreuer know?

### Takeaway
- Since the Betreuungsrechtsreform (1 Jan 2023), **§ 1831 Abs. 4 BGB** (formerly § 1906 Abs. 4 BGB) requires **prior approval by the Betreuungsgericht** if a Betreuter living in a hospital, Heim or *sonstige Einrichtung* — which includes a group home in the Eingliederungshilfe — is to be deprived of liberty **"durch mechanische Vorrichtungen, Medikamente oder auf andere Weise über einen längeren Zeitraum oder regelmäßig"**.
- Medication counts as such a measure when it is given **with the aim of sedation / restricting movement (Ruhigstellung)**. It does *not* count when sedation is merely a side effect of a therapeutic indication; that boundary is contested.
- Crucially, the betreuungsrechtliche justification is limited to **danger to self (Selbstgefährdung)** or a necessary treatment. **Protection of others (Fremdgefährdung) is not a ground under § 1831 BGB**. It falls under the Länder PsychKGs and criminal/police law.
- **§ 1832 BGB (ärztliche Zwangsmaßnahmen)** is a separate, stricter regime. It is currently being reformed: the BVerfG ruled on 26 Nov 2024 (1 BvL 1/24) and Parliament has a deadline of 31 Dec 2026. The government bill BT-Drs. 21/7561 had a 1st reading on 24 Sep 2026 and an expert hearing on 7 Oct 2026, but was not yet passed per available sources.
- The UN CRPD Committee (2023) expressed concern about chemical restraint against persons with intellectual disabilities in Germany.

### Cited Findings
**Statute (§ 1831 BGB):**
- § 1831 BGB is the successor provision to § 1906 BGB (in force since 1 Jan 2023 with the Betreuungsrechtsreform). Its title is "Freiheitsentziehende Unterbringung und freiheitsentziehende Maßnahmen" (Law) — [dejure.org § 1831 BGB](https://dejure.org/gesetze/BGB/1831.html); [JuraForum § 1831](https://www.juraforum.de/gesetze/bgb/1831-freiheitsentziehende-unterbringung-und-freiheitsentziehende-massnahmen); [lexetius § 1831](https://lexetius.com/BGB/1831); [lxgesetze](https://lxgesetze.de/bgb/1831)
- § 1831 Abs. 4 covers Betreute "die sich in einem Krankenhaus, Heim oder einer sonstigen Einrichtung aufhalten, denen durch Medikamente die Freiheit über einen längeren Zeitraum oder regelmäßig entzogen werden soll" (search summary of the statutory text) — [dejure.org](https://dejure.org/gesetze/BGB/1831.html)
- **[TK] Full wording as remembered:** "(4) Die Absätze 1 bis 3 gelten entsprechend, wenn dem Betreuten, der sich in einem Krankenhaus, einem Heim oder einer sonstigen Einrichtung aufhält, durch mechanische Vorrichtungen, Medikamente oder auf andere Weise über einen längeren Zeitraum oder regelmäßig die Freiheit entzogen werden soll."
  - Abs. 1 = Zulässigkeit only zum Wohl des Betreuten:
    - Nr. 1: Gefahr der Selbsttötung oder erheblichen gesundheitlichen Selbstschädigung;
    - Nr. 2: notwendige Untersuchung/Heilbehandlung, die ohne Unterbringung nicht durchgeführt werden kann, bei fehlender Einsichtsfähigkeit.
  - Abs. 2 = Genehmigungsvorbehalt. Without approval the measure is allowed only if delay would be dangerous ("Gefahr im Verzug"), and approval must be obtained "unverzüglich" afterwards.
  - Abs. 3 = the Betreuer must end the measure when its conditions cease and notify the court.
  - Abs. 5 = an authorised representative (Bevollmächtigter) needs a written power of attorney that expressly covers these measures.

**Medication as a freiheitsentziehende Maßnahme (FEM):**
- The Lexikon Betreuungsrecht lists "sedierende Medikamente" alongside Bettgitter, Stecktische, Festbinden and complex door-locking devices as measures requiring approval in clinics and Heime. It notes that it is **disputed** whether medication not given *for* sedation, but restricting the urge to move as a **side effect**, is included — [Lexikon Betreuungsrecht: Unterbringungsähnliche Maßnahme](https://www.lexikon-betreuungsrecht.de/Unterbringungs%C3%A4hnliche_Ma%C3%9Fnahme); [Lexikon: Freiheitsentziehende Maßnahme](https://www.lexikon-betreuungsrecht.de/Freiheitsentziehende_Ma%C3%9Fnahme)

  - FEM exists where an agent is used *gezielt* to prevent leaving or moving.
  - It does not exist if the aim is a therapeutic effect and the reduced urge to move is a side effect.
  - A practice portal (SKFM) asks whether "nicht der therapeutische Zweck, sondern die Ruhigstellung im Vordergrund steht".
  - A practice portal cites ~24 hours as a guideline value for "längerer Zeitraum". This is uncertain and not authoritative.
  - [SKFM Neunkirchen](https://skfm-nk.de/index.php/freiheitsentziehende-massnahmen/); [jurahilfe FEM](https://www.jurahilfe.de/blog/freiheitsentziehende-massnahmen)

  - **BGH XII ZB 24/12 (27 Jun 2012):** Bettgitter and fixation in a chair with a pelvic belt are FEM under § 1906 Abs. 4 (old). A Vorsorgevollmacht does **not** dispense with court approval (Law) — [rewis](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/dvn-27-06-2012-xii-zb-2412/)

This includes measures under § 1831 Abs. 4 BGB regardless of intrusiveness; the example given is bed rails. The Bundesrat (BR-Drs. 329/26, 29 May 2026) proposed dropping the Verfahrenspfleger for certain measures, and the Federal Government rejected this — [BT-Drs. 21/7561](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/075/2107561.pdf); [BR-Drs. 329/26](https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2026/0329-26.pdf); [Bundestag summary of BR-Drs. 329/26](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1194332/21-26-53-4.pdf)

**§ 1832 BGB (ärztliche Zwangsmaßnahmen) and the current reform:**
- A medical measure is an ärztliche Zwangsmaßnahme only if it contradicts the Betreuter's **natürlicher Wille** (§ 1832 Abs. 1 S. 1). Per **BGH XII ZB 60/26 (29 Apr 2026)**, only the ability to express a natural will is required (Law) — [rewis XII ZB 60/26](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/q8l-29-04-2026-xii-zb-6026/)
- The requirements are **cumulative**:
  - necessity to avert an imminent considerable health harm (Nr. 1);
  - a **serious attempt to convince the person** without pressure (Nr. 4; BGH XII ZB 334/25, 29 Oct 2025);
  - no milder means (Nr. 5);
  - benefit clearly outweighing harm (Nr. 6).
  - Plus court approval (§ 1832 Abs. 2).
  - Currently only during an inpatient hospital stay (Krankenhausvorbehalt) (Law)
  - [rewis XII ZB 334/25](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/vgt-29-10-2025-xii-zb-33425/); [lxgesetze § 1832](https://lxgesetze.de/bgb/1832); [lexetius § 1832](https://lexetius.com/BGB/1832,2)
- **BVerfG, 26 Nov 2024, 1 BvL 1/24:** the exceptionless Krankenhausvorbehalt is incompatible with the Grundgesetz. It continues to apply in the interim, and the legislature must reform by **31 Dec 2026** (Law) — [rewis BVerfG](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/r4b-26-11-2024-1-bvl-124/)
- **Government bill "Gesetz zur Änderung der Regelung über ärztliche Zwangsmaßnahmen im Betreuungsrecht und zur Stärkung des ultima-ratio-Gebots"** (BR-Drs. 329/26; BT-Drs. 21/7561):
  - It would allow coercive treatment outside hospital in narrowly limited exceptional cases, with the hospital stay remaining the rule.
  - 1st reading 24 Sep 2026; hearing of 7 experts on 7 Oct 2026. Experts said parts did not go far enough.
  - The Bundesrat wanted the approval period extended from 6 weeks to 3 months; the government called this "zu weitgehend".
  - **No source found confirming final passage** as of the search date.
  - [Bundestag text archive KW41/2026](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw41-pa-recht-betreuungsrecht-1201714); [Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/news/betreuungsrecht-anderungen-bei-arztlichen-zwangsmassnahmen-angeregt-82fed3f3-8909-4e83-b245-2ea0359ee1a9); [ad-hoc-news on hearing](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/wirtschaft/aerztliche-zwangsmassnahmen-sieben-sachverstaendige-fordern-nachbesserungen/70291384); [Bundesärztekammer statement](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/66/aa/717649/Stellungnahme-Gutachten-SG2603310172.pdf)

**Werdenfelser Weg:**
- A procedural approach within existing Betreuungsrecht, originating at AG Garmisch-Partenkirchen. It aims to reduce FEM (bed rails, belts) through a structured decision process with all parties, using **specially trained Verfahrenspfleger** (with a care background) — [AG Garmisch-Partenkirchen info](https://www.justiz.bayern.de/gerichte-und-behoerden/amtsgerichte/garmisch-partenkirchen/info_service_1.php); [IWW article](https://www.iww.de/sr/heimpflegebetreuung/betreuung-werdenfelser-weg-effektiv-ueberfluessige-freiheitsentziehende-massnahmen-vermeiden-f85300); [Bayern Kurzbeschreibung](https://www.bayern.verfassungsgerichtshof.de/media/images/behoerden-und-gerichte/kurzbeschreibung_werdenfelser_weg.pdf)
- AG Augsburg reported a decline of **>35%** in court-approved restraints in homes after adopting it in 2012 (Obs) — [AG Augsburg press release 2015](https://www.justiz.bayern.de/gerichte-und-behoerden/amtsgerichte/augsburg/presse/2015/7.php)
- It also explicitly covers **facilities for people with disabilities (Eingliederungshilfe)**, e.g. in Köln and Bonn — [Stadt Köln brochure](https://www.stadt-koeln.de/mediaasset/content/pdf50/werdenfelser_weg_bfrei.pdf); [Bonn ULB document](https://epflicht.ulb.uni-bonn.de/download/pdf/55843); [Justiz NRW "Selbstbestimmung stärken – Fixierung vermeiden"](https://www.justiz.nrw/BS/lebenslagen/familie/Betreuungsverfahren/alternative_freiheitsentz/index.php)
- **Home setting:** the Bundestag Scientific Service addressed FEM in **home care (häusliche Pflege)**. [TK] The Genehmigungsvorbehalt of § 1906 Abs. 4 (old) / § 1831 Abs. 4 generally does *not* apply when relatives care at home, only in Einrichtungen — [WD 7-255-06](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/408122/8740e942c4c80ed8e360350186eaf506/wd-7-255-06-pdf-data.pdf)

### Inferences
**What a family/Betreuer must know.** Items 1–3 rest on cited sources; items 4–9 are [TK]-based legal reasoning to be checked by a lawyer or Betreuungsgericht.
1. If the group home (an "Einrichtung") is to give medication **with the purpose of calming/sedating him to restrict his movement**, regularly or for a longer period, the **Betreuer must apply to the Betreuungsgericht for approval *beforehand*** (§ 1831 Abs. 4 BGB). This applies to both regular and repeatedly used PRN (Bedarfsmedikation) given "zur Ruhigstellung". A Vorsorgevollmacht does not replace court approval (BGH XII ZB 24/12, by analogy).
2. **Medication prescribed for a diagnosed condition** (e.g. anxiety disorder, ADHD) with sedation only as a side effect is generally *not* a FEM, but this boundary is contested. Documenting a clear **therapeutic indication and target symptoms** — rather than "Ruhigstellung" — is therefore both medically and legally important.
3. Because § 1831 only justifies measures for the **person's own welfare** (Selbstgefährdung/necessary treatment), sedation **purely to protect residents and staff** cannot be approved under Betreuungsrecht. Acute danger to others is handled by:
   - de-escalation and emergency measures (Notwehr/Nothilfe § 32 StGB, Notstand § 34 StGB [TK]);
   - PsychKG of the respective Land, e.g. PsychKG NRW or BayPsychKHG [TK];
   - crisis services and psychiatric admission.
   Group homes should have a written **crisis plan**.
4. **Ordinary consent:** for a psychotropic that is not a FEM and not coercive, the **Betreuer with the Aufgabenbereich Gesundheitssorge** gives informed consent if the patient is not einwilligungsfähig [TK].
   - It must follow the patient's wishes and presumed will (§ 1821 BGB, unterstützte Entscheidungsfindung [TK]).
   - It must follow physician information duties (§ 630e BGB, incl. Abs. 5: explaining essentials to the patient himself in an appropriate way [TK]).
   - Court approval under **§ 1829 BGB** is needed only for risk of death or of severe, long-lasting harm [TK].
5. **If he resists** taking medication (natural will against it), covert or forced administration is an **ärztliche Zwangsmaßnahme (§ 1832 BGB)**. It is subject to the strict cumulative criteria, court approval and (currently) the **hospital requirement** — it is *not* possible in the group home under current law. The reform pending at end-2026 may permit narrowly defined exceptions outside hospitals; its final content must be checked.
6. Approvals are time-limited (max. 1–2 years) and must be ended by the Betreuer as soon as the conditions cease (§ 1831 Abs. 3 [TK]). Documentation of use (PRN log) is essential.
7. At home with family, § 1831 Abs. 4 generally does not apply [TK]; on moving into a group home it does.
8. Ethical framing: UN-CRPD concern about chemical restraint, NICE NG11 and the Ulm catalogue all point the same way. Any calming medication should be:
   - the least restrictive option;
   - indication-based;
   - time-limited;
   - reviewed;
   - embedded in a behaviour-support plan;
   - subject to a **regular deprescribing review**.
9. The Werdenfelser Weg model can be explicitly requested or welcomed in court proceedings.

## 7. German care structures and practical pathways for adults with ID and complex challenging behaviour

### Takeaway
Germany has the following specialised structures:
- **MZEBs (§ 119c SGB V)**, interdisciplinary outpatient centres for adults with ID/severe multiple disabilities: 67 authorised, 64 active per a Federal Government answer of uncertain date.
- Specialised psychiatric services, notably **Prof. Tanja Sappok's Universitätsklinik für Inklusive Medizin, Krankenhaus Mara, Bielefeld-Bethel** (since 2023; previously KEH Berlin).
- **Intensive residential care (Intensivwohnen)** in the Eingliederungshilfe, accessed via the Gesamtplanverfahren of the Eingliederungshilfeträger.

A practical pathway for this family:
- a medical work-up via the GP and an MZEB;
- psychiatric/behavioural diagnostics, including FBA and SEED, at a specialised ID-psychiatry service;
- a behaviour-support plan and staff coaching;
- needs assessment in the Gesamtplanverfahren for an appropriately staffed group home;
- legal set-up of Betreuung covering Gesundheitssorge and Aufenthaltsbestimmung.

### Inferences
**Practical pathway for the family** (synthesis; [TK] for BTHG details):
1. **Medical work-up** via the GP/Hausarzt and an **MZEB**. Check MZEB eligibility (GdB/Merkzeichen) in advance. Areas to cover:
   - GI (constipation, GERD);
   - dental;
   - ENT;
   - eyes;
   - orthopaedics;
   - sleep laboratory/OSA screening;
   - weight/metabolic baseline before any antipsychotic.
2. **Specialised ID psychiatry/psychology:**
   - psychiatric differential diagnosis with DM-ID-2-type criteria;
   - **SEED-2** emotional-development profile;
   - a formal **FBA**.
   Options include the Universitätsklinik für Inklusive Medizin (Bielefeld-Bethel) or regional centres for ID mental health, often attached to MZEBs or psychiatric hospitals.
3. A **written behaviour support plan** covering:
   - function-based strategies (FCT, DRA, consistent responses across mother/grandmother/staff);
   - AAC (UK-Beratung);
   - a visual daily structure;
   - an activity-rich programme;
   - food management (structured meals, no "negotiation" over sweets, non-food reinforcers);
   - a crisis/de-escalation plan.
   It should be agreed **before** the move and transferred to the group-home team, with coaching.
4. **Eingliederungshilfe (SGB IX):** request a **Gesamtplanverfahren** [TK: §§ 117 ff. SGB IX] that documents the intensive support need (staffing ratio, Fachdienst, possibly Intensivwohngruppe) so the group home is resourced adequately.
5. **Legal set-up:**
   - Betreuung (or Vorsorgevollmacht) with Gesundheitssorge, Aufenthaltsbestimmung and, if needed, the explicit power for § 1831 measures;
   - an agreement with the home on PRN rules;
   - court approval if medication is used for Ruhigstellung;
   - a periodic medication review (target symptoms, side effects, deprescribing).
6. **Peer support:** Angelman e.V. (angelman.de) for family networks and adult-specific experience [TK — no content retrieved]. Also ASF (USA) resources such as the Behavior Series and the Physician's Note — [ASF Physician's note](https://angelman.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Physicians-Note-Symptom-Treatment-Checklist-AS-for-IEP.pdf).
