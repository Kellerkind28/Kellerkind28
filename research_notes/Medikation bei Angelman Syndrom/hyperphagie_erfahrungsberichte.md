# Hyperphagia, food-seeking, obesity and food-related aggression in Angelman syndrome (esp. paternal UPD 15), appetite-modifying pharmacotherapy, and caregiver experience reports on psychotropic/sleep medications (status: November 2026)

Patient context (for the report writer): male, 26 y, 164 cm, 82 kg (BMI ~30.5 = Adipositas Grad I), AS due to paternal UPD 15, no epilepsy, hard stools, avoids exercise. Strong sugar craving, aggression when sweets are refused, very selective/carbohydrate-heavy eating, attention-seeking aggression, restlessness/ADHD-like signs, fragmented sleep.

Evidence labels used throughout:

## 1. How common are obesity and hyperphagia in AS, by genotype (especially UPD and imprinting defect), and what mechanisms are proposed?

### Takeaway
Obesity and hyperphagic or food-seeking behaviour are clearly more frequent in AS than in the general population. Several older and small studies link them most strongly to the non-deletion genotypes paternal UPD and imprinting defect (ID); for UPD the onset is often in early childhood. The largest recent paediatric study (n=145) found moderate hyperphagia across all genotypes without a significant genotype effect. So the UPD/ID–obesity link is plausible and repeatedly reported, but not uniformly confirmed. The mechanism is unexplained; the leading hypothesis is a double dose of paternally expressed 15q11-q13 genes in UPD/ID.

### Cited Findings
- **Genotype distribution:** ~80% of AS cases are due to maternal 15q11-q13 deletion, ~7% to paternal UPD, ~10% to UBE3A mutation and ~3% to imprinting defects; ~90% of ID cases are sporadic primary epimutations [PR, review/case report, AJMG A] — [Wiley AJMG A 173(3):753-757, doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.38072](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/10.1002@ajmg.a.38072.pdf)
- **Mosaic ID / "Prader-Willi-like" phenotype:** patients with mosaic imprinting defects "present with a milder phenotype, often with hyperphagia and obesity or non-specific intellectual disability" [PR, case report + review] — [AJMG A 2017;173(3):753-757](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/ajmg.a.38072)
- **Gillessen-Kaesbach et al. 1999 (Eur J Hum Genet):** described patients with obesity, muscular hypotonia and comparatively mild intellectual disability (some with speech) who were **initially suspected of having Prader-Willi syndrome** but had an AS imprinting defect, calling it "a previously unrecognised phenotype". The authors suggested an incomplete imprinting defect or cellular mosaicism as the explanation. Title: "A previously unrecognised phenotype characterised by obesity, muscular hypotonia, and ability to speak in patients with Angelman syndrome caused by an imprinting defect" [PR, case series, n=7] — [Univ. Lübeck research portal](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/a-previously-unrecognised-phenotype-characterised-by-obesity-musc/); [PubMed 10424818 (listed in search)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10424818/)
- **Lossie et al. 2001 (J Med Genet 38:834-845):** 104 patients from 93 families with classical AS, assigned to molecular classes I–V and compared on 22 clinical variables including growth [PR, cohort]. The obesity-by-class results were **not retrieved** (see Gaps) — [PMC1734773 record](https://vufind.lboro.ac.uk/PrimoRecord/cdi_pubmedcentral_primary_oai_pubmedcentral_nih_gov_1734773)
- **Brennan et al. 2015 (AJMG A 167A(1):142-146, PMID 25402239):** "Increased body mass in infancy and early toddlerhood in Angelman syndrome patients with uniparental disomy and imprinting center defects." The study was prompted by four cases of infantile obesity in non-deletion AS. Body mass can already be raised in the first year of life, and the authors recommend considering AS in obese infants or toddlers with developmental delay. They state that "a mechanism explaining the association of UPD and IC defects with obesity has not been identified" [PR, cohort] — [BVS record mdl-25402239](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/oncologiauy/resource/en/mdl-25402239)
- **Earlier natural-history analysis (~2013):** participants with UPD/ID were heavier than the general population, and **6/13 (46%) with UPD/ID were obese** [PR; authorship not verified in this session] — [PMC3563320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3563320)
- **Mertz et al. 2014 (Res Dev Disabil 35(11):2681-2690, doi:10.1016/j.ridd.2014.07.025):** Danish case series, n=39, eating behaviour by questionnaire, growth from records. **Eating behaviour and pre-/postnatal growth depend on genotype.** Children with paternal UPD had more severe eating behaviour and higher obesity risk than children with deletions. Deletion and UBE3A-mutation groups did not differ. Apparently normal head growth in UBE3A and UPD patients probably reflects generally increased growth [PR; details partly from secondary summaries] — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40gKNKW7); [Behaviorist Book Club summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/eating-behavior--prenatal-and-postnatal-growth-in-angelman-syndrome/); [FIND resources summary](https://findresources.co.uk/eating-behavior-prenatal-and-postnatal-growth-in-angelman-syndrome). Secondary summaries put the onset of obesity at about age 2–3 [SEK]
- **"Hyperphagia, Growth, and Puberty in Children with Angelman Syndrome" (J Clin Med 2023;12(18):5981, PMID 37762921):** n=145 children. Moderate hyperphagia (mean Dykens Hyperphagia Questionnaire score 25, range 11–55) and lower height SDS. BMI-SDS was higher than norm data, **with the deviation increasing with age**. Parents reported hyperphagia in about one third of children, **in both deletion and non-deletion subtypes; no genotype association** [PR, cohort; authors not verified in this session] — [PubMed 37762921](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37762921/); [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2077-0383/12/18/5981)
- **Adults, Dutch cohort (2015–2018 data, n=95, age 18–83, mean 31.6 y):** more than half had normal BMI, **37% were overweight (BMI ≥25)**, and about one third of those were obese (BMI ≥30) [PR via SEK summary; primary paper probably den Besten et al., AJMG A 2021, not verified] — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/study-details-challenges-facing-adults-with-angelman/)
- **Adolescents/adults, caregiver interviews (n=110):** **32% overweight or obese**, with obesity disproportionately affecting women. Genotypes: deletion 68%, UPD 9%, UBE3A 8% [PR; likely Larson et al. "Angelman syndrome in adulthood", AJMG A 2015] — [JCI Insight reference listing](https://insight.jci.org/references/scholar/4097/B47); [PMC5534346 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5534346/table/T1)
- A 134-patient study found that UBE3A mutations or UPD/ID were associated with **higher rates of obesity** and less frequently with seizures, hypopigmentation and microcephaly [PR, cited in review; primary paper not identified] — [search snippet from review, see PMC11404063](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11404063)
- A review of non-deletion cases states that UPD patients have milder symptoms and **higher BMI** than other genetic subtypes [PR review] — [search snippet, see PMC3846355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3846355)
- **Welham et al. 2015 (AJMG A 167A(3):572-578, PMID 25691410), "Are Angelman and Prader-Willi syndromes more similar than we thought? Food-related behavior problems in Angelman, Cornelia de Lange, Fragile X, Prader-Willi and 1p36 deletion syndromes":** questionnaire, ~152–156 participants. PWS scored highest overall, but **"people with AS were reported to display food-related problems at least as severe as those with PWS in some areas"**: taking/storing food, negative behaviours, impaired satiety and food preoccupation [PR, cross-syndrome questionnaire] — [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/are-angelman-and-prader-willi-syndromes-more-similar-than-we-thou); [BVS mdl-25691410](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-25691410)
- **Keute et al. 2021 (Mol Psychiatry 26:3625-3633):** natural-history data, 250 individuals and 848 assessments. Clinical severity differs by genotype: deletion carriers are the most impaired, and UBE3A-variant and ID carriers are less affected than UPD carriers (according to the snippet). **No BMI or obesity results were found in the retrieved snippets** [PR] — [PMC8505254](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8505254/); [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8z43c9pr/qt8z43c9pr.pdf)
- **Mechanism hypothesis (Salminen, Crespi & Mokkonen 2019):** "an increased dosage of paternally expressed genes in the UPD and ID AS genotypes may explain why increased food selectivity, hyperphagic behaviors, and higher BMI levels are seen". They propose **opposite** food phenotypes in AS and PWS: hedonic food reward possibly increased in AS and decreased in PWS. This is a theoretical/evolutionary review, not tested experimentally [PR review, hypothesis] — [findresources summary](https://findresources.co.uk/food-related-issues-in-angelman-syndromes); [Angelman Syndrome News on "baby foods" review](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/poor-sleep-preference-for-baby-foods-associated-with-angelman-syndrome-review-says/); [PMC6633366](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6633366)
- A secondary source states that obesity is more frequent among infants and toddlers with paternal UPD or imprinting defects than among those with UBE3A mutations [SEK] — [Angelman Syndrome News, "Angelman Syndrome and Obesity"](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/health-insights/angelman-syndrome-and-obesity/)
- The ERN-ITHACA webinar "Angelman syndrome in clinical practice" (13 May 2025) covers clinical management, including obesity [REG/educational; contents not retrieved] — [ERN-ITHACA PDF](https://ern-ithaca.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025-05-13-Webinar_Angelman-syndrome-in-clinical-practice_Final-Version.pdf)

### Inferences
- For this patient (paternal UPD, adult, BMI 30.5), obesity matches a well-described genotype-associated pattern rather than being only a parenting or diet issue. This supports framing it medically as a partly biological drive toward food (hyperphagia/food reward) combined with learned behaviour.
- In paternal UPD, paternally expressed genes such as SNRPN, MAGEL2, NDN and SNORD116 are present in double dose, which is the genetic opposite of PWS (where they are missing). The fact that both conditions can show hyperphagia is therefore paradoxical. Because the mechanism differs from PWS, PWS-proven drugs cannot simply be assumed to work in AS-UPD.
- The UPD subtype tends to have better motor function and less severe epilepsy (this patient has none). That brings greater mobility and a better ability to get at food, which may raise food-seeking opportunities.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Lossie 2001, Mertz 2014 and the J Clin Med 2023 paper were not accessible (PMC blocked), so exact genotype-specific obesity percentages could not be verified.
- No adult BMI data broken down by genotype (UPD vs deletion) were found.
- No study directly examines reward-circuit or dopaminergic mechanisms of food craving in AS (e.g., imaging or UBE3A in hypothalamic or striatal circuits). Animal-model data on UBE3A and feeding were not searched.
- Brennan et al. 2015 cohort size and exact numbers were not retrieved.

## 2. Food selectivity (ARFID-like eating) in AS and ID, and feeding-therapy approaches

### Takeaway
Feeding problems are very common in AS. A registry study found feeding problems in 83% of adults, most often food-seeking. A narrow food repertoire and preference for soft or "baby" foods are typical, and in UPD/ID food selectivity may be combined with hyperphagia. No AS-specific feeding-therapy trials were found; the approaches have to be transferred from autism/ID practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Cassidy et al. 2025 (AJMG A), "Association Between Feeding Problems and Gastrointestinal Symptoms" in adults with AS:** Global Angelman Syndrome Registry, caregiver report, n=57 adults. **83% had feeding problems; the most common was food-seeking behaviour.** Feeding problems were associated with GI symptoms and with language/communication, but these variables did not significantly predict feeding problems [PR, registry] — [UEL repository](https://repository.uel.ac.uk/item/8zwyz); [PDF](https://repository.uel.ac.uk/download/e9ec6a23a7e1c7788313177da46df49dcf6bc4fda6f7c6049bf63a13d78d66ec/373951/American%20J%20of%20Med%20Genetics%20Pt%20A%20-%202025%20-%20Cassidy%20-%20Association%20Between%20Feeding%20Problems%20and%20Gastrointestinal%20Symptoms%20.pdf)
- More than 60% of children with AS show a range of eating problems and **a narrow range of food preferences**. Food-related difficulties may lead to higher rates of obesity [SEK, Cerebra/FIND syndrome sheet] — [FIND resources "Feeding Difficulties in Angelman Syndrome"](https://findresources.co.uk/the-syndromes/angelman/behaviourfood)
- Dietary interventions are hard to apply in AS because children tend to be picky eaters and are easily sickened by certain foods [SEK] — [FIND resources](https://findresources.co.uk/the-syndromes/angelman/behaviourfood)
- Salminen et al. 2019 describe "hyperphagic food selectivity" in AS-UPD/ID and a preference for "baby foods" (soft, sweet) [PR review, summarised in SEK] — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/poor-sleep-preference-for-baby-foods-associated-with-angelman-syndrome-review-says/)
- Longitudinal natural-history data on growth and feeding challenges in AS (Boston/UCSD) were presented at the Gatlinburg Conference 2025 [ABS] — [Gatlinburg abstract](https://gatlinburg.lsi.ku.edu/?p=2460)
- A case report highlights the need for nutritional intervention research in AS [SEK] — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/case-report-highlights-need-nutritional-intervention-research-angelman/)

### Inferences
- The patient's profile (rejects new foods, accepts mainly carbohydrates and sugar, aggression on refusal) fits both the AS-typical narrow repertoire and a reward-driven sugar preference. Behavioural feeding approaches from autism/ID practice are applicable but were not verified as sources in this session. They include gradual exposure and "food chaining" (stepwise expansion from accepted foods), predictable meal structure, and swapping toward lower-sugar versions of accepted foods.
- Because food refusal and food craving coexist, sudden blanket restriction of sweets risks escalating aggression. A gradual, predictable approach (see section 3) is preferable.

### Gaps
- No AS-specific study on ARFID diagnosis or feeding-therapy outcomes (e.g., behavioural feeding intervention, food chaining) was found.
- No study specifically on sugar craving (as opposed to general hyperphagia) in AS was found.

## 3. Dietary approaches (LGIT, ketogenic diet) and practical environmental food management (adapted from Prader-Willi practice)

### Takeaway
LGIT and ketogenic diets in AS are studied mainly for **seizures**. Reports of behaviour, cognition or sleep benefits rest on case series and secondary summaries without hard data, and LGIT can worsen constipation. The structured food-security approach from PWS ("No doubt + No hope = No disappointment": locked storage, fixed meal and snack times, no unplanned food) is the best-described environmental strategy. It is directly relevant to food-related aggression, although it is evaluated in PWS, not AS.

### Cited Findings
- **Thibert et al. 2012 (Epilepsia):** first study of low glycemic index treatment (LGIT) for seizures in AS, ASF-funded; seizure reduction "up to 90%" (foundation press material) [PR; the ASF page appears to give a different year, see Gaps] — [ASF LGIT resource](https://angelman.org/resources/lgit-and-ketogenic-diets-for-angelman-syndrome); [angelman.org](https://angelman.org/?p=2296)
- **Grocott OR, Herrington KS, Pfeifer HH, Thiele EA, Thibert RL. Epilepsy Behav 2017;68:45-50, doi:10.1016/j.yebeh.2016.12.018:** LGIT case series from the MGH Center for Dietary Therapy, **23 patients aged 2–31**, average ~3 years on the diet. 5 became seizure-free, 10 had seizures only during illness or only non-convulsive seizures, and 7 had fewer seizures; 4 improved on diet alone. The news summary says the team reported improved **cognition, physical function and behaviour**. Adverse effects were mild [PR; behaviour claim from SEK, not verified with data] — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/angelman-seizures-decline-with-low-blood-sugar-diet/); [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4No8MyOl)
- **DIANE study (2025):** low-glycemic-index ketogenic diet vs usual care in children with AS, assessing neurodevelopmental **and sleep** outcomes. Results not retrieved [PR] — [PMC12538946](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12538946/); [repositorio.uax.es](https://repositorio.uax.es/handle/20.500.12080/51014)
- **LGIT and constipation:** a Thibert-group GI study found that the low glycemic index diet (used by 16 patients) **can worsen or trigger constipation** in AS [PR] — [Angelman Syndrome News on GI study](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/gastrointestinal-issues-prevalence-angelman-syndrome-constipation-gastroesophageal-reflux-disease/)
- A randomised trial of ketogenic diet/medical nutrition and a trial of exogenous ketones in AS exist (Vanderbilt). Results were not retrieved [ABS/SEK] — [AES abstract, exogenous ketones RCT](https://aesnet.org/abstractslisting/use-of-exogenous-ketones-as-a-nutritional-approach-for-patients-with-angelman-syndrome--early-results-of-a-randomized-control-trial); [VUMC news 2019](https://news.vumc.org/2019/05/16/nutritional-intervention-for-angelman-syndrome-studied/); [Matthew's Friends](https://www.matthewsfriends.org/keto-therapies/keto-therapies-for/angelman-syndrome)
- **PWS food-security principle (PWSA USA):** access to food creates hope, hope creates anxiety, and anxiety can lead to unwanted behaviour. **Removing access to food is presented as a way to reduce behaviour problems.** Motto: **"NO DOUBT + NO HOPE = NO DISAPPOINTMENT"**. Meals are served consistently at a table, on plates [REG/guidance, patient organisation] — [PWSA "Follow the Principles of Food Security" (2022 revision)](https://www.pwsausa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Follow-the-Principles-of-Food-Security-2022-revision-Branded.pdf); [PWSA 2022 PDF](https://www.pwsausa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Follow-the-Principles-of-Food-Security.pdf)
- **Locks:** families lock the refrigerator, pantry and kitchen cabinets. Some use an enclosed locked pantry; in open-plan kitchens, families often lock just the few cabinets that hold all the food. Some add motion alarms [guidance] — [PWSA "Food Security for Persons with PWS"](https://www.pwsausa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Food-Security-for-Persons-with-PWS.pdf)
- **Origin and definition:** the principle comes from Janice Forster MD and Linda Gourash MD. Food security means **no doubt** about when and what food is coming, **no hope** of extra or unplanned food, and **no disappointment**, because expectations are clear and consistently followed [guidance] — [FPWR "Hyperphagia in PWS"](https://www.fpwr.org/hubfs/Resources_Assets/FPWR%20Resource%20-%20Hyperphagia%20in%20PWS.pdf)
- **Validated measurement:** the Vanderbilt "Food Safe Zone" questionnaire lists common strategies: locking food sources, supervising whenever food is present, coordinating intake with school or day programmes, and educating family and neighbours [PR/preprint, J Neurodev Disord 2024] — [medRxiv 2024.01.26.24301844](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.26.24301844v1.full); [J Neurodev Disord s11689-024-09589-y](https://biomedcentral-preview-jneurodevdisorders.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/counter/pdf/10.1186/s11689-024-09589-y.pdf)
- PWSA guidance on PWS and food theft [guidance] — [PWSA "PWS and Theft"](https://www.pwsausa.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/PWS-and-Theft-ED-1080.pdf)

### Inferences
- For food-related aggression, the PWS logic transfers directly to this patient: aggression when sweets are refused is partly driven by uncertainty and "hope". A fully predictable structure (fixed times, a visual schedule, a sweet planned at a fixed time instead of negotiated) lowers the number of refusal situations. This is untested in AS, so it is an analogy.
- A full LGIT or ketogenic diet is hard for a very selective, carbohydrate-preferring eater and can worsen existing hard stools. A practical compromise is to gradually swap high-GI sweets for lower-GI or sugar-reduced alternatives within his accepted foods. This is not an evidence-based protocol.
- Adults have a right to self-determination, so locking food has to be discussed with the legal guardian (rechtliche Betreuung). In Germany, locking measures in residential settings can count as freedom-restricting measures (freiheitsentziehende Maßnahmen); this legal point was not researched.

### Gaps
- No controlled data on LGIT effects on behaviour, sleep or appetite in AS were verified. The Grocott 2017 behaviour statement comes only from a news summary.
- Year discrepancy: the ASF/search snippet described the Thibert LGIT study as a "2024 report in Epilepsia". The original LGIT paper is commonly cited as Thibert et al., Epilepsia 2012;53(9):1498-1502, which matches the task brief. This could not be resolved.
- DIANE study results were not retrieved.
- The PWS "green/yellow/red" colour system mentioned in some PWS materials was not found in the PWSA documents retrieved.
- German-language food-security guidance (e.g., from the Prader-Willi-Syndrom Vereinigung Deutschland) was not searched.

## 4. Appetite- and weight-reducing drugs: evidence in PWS, genetic and hypothalamic obesity, ID, and AS

### Takeaway
**There are no published data on GLP-1 RAs, tirzepatide, metformin, topiramate, DCCR or setmelanotide in AS** (no case report found). The best adjacent evidence comes from PWS:
- liraglutide: an RCT in adolescents showed no significant BMI-SDS effect, but hyperphagia scores were lower;
- semaglutide: small case series show variable weight loss of up to ~14%, with reported satiety;
- topiramate: the TOPRADER RCT improved eating-behaviour scores;
- metformin: an open-label pilot study suggests less food-related anxiety and distress.

Case reports in Smith-Magenis syndrome and in an ID patient with holoprosencephaly describe less compulsive eating and **less aggression** on semaglutide. Diazoxide choline is FDA-approved for PWS hyperphagia, but its EU application was **withdrawn in April 2026**. Setmelanotide does not fit AS (it is limited to defects in the leptin–melanocortin pathway).

### Cited Findings

**GLP-1 receptor agonists**
- **Liraglutide in PWS, RCT (J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2022, PMID 36181471):** multicentre, placebo/no-treatment-controlled, n=55 children and adolescents with PWS and obesity. **Primary endpoint (BMI-SDS) not met** (estimated difference −0.14 in adolescents and −0.07 in children at week 52). Secondary endpoint: **adolescents on liraglutide had lower hyperphagia total and drive scores** at week 52 than no treatment, which the authors describe as a signal needing further study. Mostly GI adverse events [PR, RCT] — [PubMed 36181471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36181471); [Marmara University record](https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/3559086e-6885-4258-9f8c-9f0c350556d4/liraglutide-for-weight-management-in-children-and-adolescents-with-prader-willi-syndrome-and-obesity)
- **Semaglutide in adult PWS, long-term case series (Front Endocrinol 2025, doi:10.3389/fendo.2024.1528457, PMC11790462):** 3 non-diabetic adults followed for up to 37 months. Efficacy was variable: one patient's weight stabilised, and the others lost **up to 14.4% and 11%**. It was well tolerated, even after metabolic surgery. The authors call for RCTs [PR, case series] — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2024.1528457/text); [PMC11790462](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11790462/)
- **ECE 2025 abstract (semaglutide in PWS):** **all patients reported appetite suppression and increased satiety**, which diminished during short interruptions caused by drug shortages. "Mild to moderate efficacy" [ABS] — [Endocrine Abstracts ea0110ep926](https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0110/ea0110ep926)
- Semaglutide in PWS with type 2 diabetes: 4 patients (Spanish study 2024) [PR] — [Endocrinol Diabetes Nutr](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-endocrinologia-diabetes-nutricion-english-ed--413-pdf-download-S2530018024000222). A 33-year-old man with PWS: HbA1c fell from 11.1% to 7.2% and weight from 99.5 to 94.3 kg over 12 months [PR, case report] — [Bentham, EMIDDT](https://beta.benthamscience.com/article/123294)
- **Smith-Magenis syndrome (JCEM Case Reports):** obesity **and behavioural symptoms** improved on injectable semaglutide. Switching to oral semaglutide was followed by **a relapse of violent behaviour**, increased food intake and weight regain [PR, case report] — [Endocrine Society, JCEM Case Reports](https://www.endocrine.org/journals/jcem-case-reports/weight-management-in-a-patient-with-smith-magenis-syndrome)
- **24-year-old woman with holoprosencephaly and a neurodevelopmental disorder:** ~18% weight loss over 1 year on semaglutide. The authors suggest GLP-1 RAs "appear to reduce compulsive eating and aggressive behaviour" [PR, case report] — [PubMed 39996062 (listed in search)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39996062/)
- MC4R heterozygous adolescent: semaglutide effective and safe (Horm Res Paediatr, doi:10.1159/000537921) [PR, case report] — [PubMed 38402868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38402868/)
- **No AS case report or trial with GLP-1 RA/semaglutide/liraglutide/tirzepatide was found** — [search across PubMed/Karger/Frontiers results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38402868/) (no AS hits)

**GLP-1 RA / metformin for antipsychotic-induced weight gain (relevant if antipsychotics are used)**
- **Network meta-analysis 2021 (PubMed 34311625):** for weight gain from atypical antipsychotics, pooled weight change was **metformin −3.0 kg (95% CI −4.22 to −1.83)** and **GLP-1 RA −3.2 kg (95% CI −5.47 to −0.96)**. The authors suggest zonisamide, metformin, GLP-1 RA and nizatidine as first-line options [PR, NMA] — [PubMed 34311625](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34311625/)
- **de Silva et al. 2016 (BMC Psychiatry, PMID 27716110):** 12 studies, 743 patients; metformin was significantly better than placebo on anthropometric and metabolic parameters [PR, meta-analysis] — [PubMed 27716110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27716110/); [PMC5048618](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5048618/)
- 2011 meta-analysis (non-diabetic patients on antipsychotics): about 4.8% weight reduction vs placebo in adults [PR] — [PubMed 20080925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20080925/)
- **2026 network meta-analysis (JAMA Psychiatry, reported July 2026):** metformin, topiramate, semaglutide, liraglutide and exenatide were the five most effective pharmacological options against antipsychotic-induced weight gain. GLP-1 RAs are a strong option once weight gain is established or when metformin is not enough [SEK, trade-press interview; primary paper not verified] — [Managed Healthcare Executive, 27 July 2026](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/five-drugs-stand-out-for-antipsychotic-induced-weight-gain-study-finds)

**Metformin**
- **Miller JL, Linville TD, Dykens EM. J Pediatr Endocrinol Metab 2014;27(1-2):23-29, doi:10.1515/jpem-2013-0116:** open-label pilot, 21 children with PWS and 6 with early-onset morbid obesity. Hyperphagia Questionnaire before and after: **both groups improved in food-related distress, anxiety and the ability to be redirected away from food**; in PWS the gains were mainly in females. Response may depend on the degree of hyperinsulinism or glucose intolerance. Uncontrolled and small [PR, open-label pilot] — [doi:10.1515/jpem-2013-0116](https://doi.org/10.1515/jpem-2013-0116); [PMC3864175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3864175)

**Topiramate**
- **Consoli A, et al. "Effect of topiramate on eating behaviours in Prader-Willi syndrome: TOPRADER double-blind randomised placebo-controlled study." Transl Psychiatry 2019 (published 4 Nov 2019), doi:10.1038/s41398-019-0597-0; NCT02810483:** n=62 (30 topiramate 50–200 mg/day, 32 placebo), 8 weeks, age 12–45, PWS with severe irritability/impulsivity, eating disorder/obesity and/or skin picking. The **primary outcome was the CGI-I responder rate** (registry). Reported finding: **eating-behaviour (DHQ) behaviour and severity scores improved significantly more on topiramate than placebo, with a significant dose–effect relationship**, still significant after adjusting for genetic subtype and hospitalisation. Planned irritability/aggression measures were the ABC irritability subscale and C-SHARP/A-SHARP [PR, RCT] — [PMC6828670](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6828670/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02810483](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02810483); [HAL](https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/hal-02409179)
- A 2023 review calls TOPRADER the only RCT so far reporting a hyporexigenic (appetite-reducing) effect in PWS, and notes "great variability both in tolerance and efficacy" of topiramate [PR review, Front Neurosci 2023] — [DOAJ Front Neurosci Feb 2023](https://doaj.org/article/65941c8d6c9c48a8a595e55791afcfda); [PMC9939745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9939745)

**Setmelanotide (Imcivree)**

- On BMI (30.5), the patient formally meets the Wegovy/Mounjaro adult label; GKV reimbursement is a separate question (section 6). Treatment would be off-evidence for AS: no AS data exist, so it would be an individual treatment trial (individueller Heilversuch) under the label, with careful monitoring.
- In an AS-UPD patient with food-related aggression, the most plausible theoretical benefit of GLP-1 RAs is lower food drive and craving. Two ID/genetic case reports (Smith-Magenis, holoprosencephaly) describe less aggression alongside less compulsive eating, which is only hypothesis-generating. Key risks: GI adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, **constipation**) in a person who cannot report symptoms verbally; lower food intake with an already restricted repertoire (risk of malnutrition or dehydration); and injections (weekly is easier than daily).
- Metformin is cheap, GKV-reimbursable for diabetes or prediabetes only (off-label for weight), and modestly effective in PWS (food anxiety) and against antipsychotic-induced weight gain. It is a pragmatic option if an antipsychotic is started, or as a first step. GI adverse effects are possible.
- Topiramate reduced eating-behaviour scores in PWS, but cognitive slowing, word-finding problems, sedation and appetite/weight loss are known class effects (not verified here). In a patient with ID and limited communication, cognitive and behavioural effects are hard to monitor.
- Setmelanotide is not indicated: AS-UPD is not a defect upstream of MC4R in the leptin–melanocortin pathway, and there are no data. Its 2026 hypothalamic-obesity indication covers acquired (e.g., post-craniopharyngioma) obesity, not genetic syndromic obesity like AS.
- DCCR is FDA-approved only for PWS and not authorised in the EU (withdrawn in 2026), so it is practically unavailable for AS in Germany and has no AS data.
- Naltrexone/bupropion: Mysimba is not marketed in Germany and is excluded as a Lifestyle-Arzneimittel. Bupropion lowers the seizure threshold, which needs consideration in AS even without epilepsy, since EEG abnormalities are common in AS (general pharmacology, not sourced here).

- TOPRADER results for **irritability/impulsivity, skin picking and the primary CGI-I outcome** were not retrieved (full text blocked). From training memory (unverified), the primary CGI-I outcome and irritability did not differ significantly; check before citing.
- No ClinicalTrials.gov search was done for ongoing GLP-1/tirzepatide trials in PWS, ID or genetic obesity (e.g., semaglutide RCTs in PWS).
- No data on tirzepatide in PWS or ID were found.
- No AS-specific data exist for any appetite-reducing drug.

## 5. Psychotropic and antiseizure drugs that markedly increase appetite or weight (risk of worsening food craving and food-related aggression)

### Takeaway
Among antipsychotics, clozapine and olanzapine cause the most weight gain. Risperidone and quetiapine have relevant appetite and weight effects. Aripiprazole is generally (though not uniformly) reported to be more weight-neutral; haloperidol, lurasidone and ziprasidone have the most favourable metabolic profiles. Mirtazapine caused hyperphagia and weight gain in an AS sleep case series, and valproate raises appetite and fat cravings. For a patient whose aggression is triggered by food, appetite-raising drugs are a specific behavioural risk, not only a metabolic one.

### Cited Findings
- **Pillinger T, et al. Lancet Psychiatry 2020;7:64-77, doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(19)30416-X, PMID 31860457:** network meta-analysis of 18 antipsychotics. Weight and BMI change ran from **haloperidol (most favourable)** to **clozapine (worst, about +3 kg vs placebo over short trials)**. Predictors of metabolic change: higher baseline weight, male sex and non-white ethnicity. Symptom improvement correlated with weight gain [PR, NMA] — [PubMed 31860457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31860457/); [BORIS Bern repository](https://boris.unibe.ch/id/eprint/137926); [Mental Elf summary](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/treatment/antipsychotics/antipsychotics-metabolic-function-schizophrenia/)
- A later paper citing Pillinger describes lurasidone and ziprasidone as showing the best metabolic changes [PR; not verified against the original] — [Tijdschrift voor Psychiatrie 2025 references](https://www.tijdschriftvoorpsychiatrie.nl/media/7/67-2025-03-spoelstra_ref.pdf)
- **Risperidone in autism:** rapid weight gain was the most common adverse event. A long-term follow-up found **more excessive appetite, more weight gain** and more enuresis with risperidone, while irritability improved [PR] — [search summary, autism trials](https://mdedge.com/content/risperidone-aripiprazole-treat-irritability-children-asd); [PMC4545698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4545698)
- **Aripiprazole in autism:** 11% discontinued for adverse events, mainly **weight gain and aggression**. A review states aripiprazole causes as much weight gain as risperidone in children with autism, while other sources describe a lower weight-gain propensity. **The sources conflict** [PR/SEK] — [Genetic Literacy Project](https://geneticliteracyproject.org/2020/03/04/once-promising-autism-drug-abilify-linked-to-heart-problems-substantial-weight-gains/); [search summary](https://mdedge.com/content/risperidone-aripiprazole-treat-irritability-children-asd)
- A pragmatic RCT comparing weight gain on risperidone vs aripiprazole in children with autism (Vanderbilt) is registered; results were not retrieved [REG] — [NCT04903353](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04903353)
- In PWS, aripiprazole is often chosen because it is less likely to cause weight gain, EPS or hyperprolactinaemia, but it is poorly investigated there [PR review] — [MDPI Brain Sci 2022;12(5):618](https://mdpi.com/2076-3425/12/5/618)
- **Mirtazapine in AS (Hanzlik E, et al. J Clin Sleep Med 2020;16(3), doi:10.5664/jcsm.8284, PMID 32022663):** retrospective chart review of 8 children with AS. Sleep latency, night waking and sleep duration improved. **"Hyperphagia and weight gain were the most significant side effects"**, leading to discontinuation in 1 patient [PR, case series] — [PubMed 32022663](https://www.pubmed.gov/32022663/); [PMC7161437](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7161437)
- **ASF management document (Feb 2025):** mirtazapine "can also be helpful in AS though can result in increased appetite and marked weight gain" [guidance, patient organisation/clinical] — [ASF Management NEM/Dystonia PDF](https://www.angelman.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ASF-Management-NEM-Dystonia.pdf)
- **Valproate:** scoping review (Popkova et al., 2026) on mechanisms of valproate-associated weight gain: hormonal and lipid mechanisms (insulin resistance/hyperinsulinaemia, hyperleptinaemia/leptin resistance) are well established, genetic pathways are emerging. Increased appetite and **craving for high-calorie foods** are reported. In a double-blind trial in 52 healthy volunteers, weight rose by +0.49 kg on valproate (not on placebo), along with **increased fast-food fat cravings**. Prevalence figures range from 10–70%, and risk is higher in women [PR] — [Scoping review (RSU)](https://science.rsu.lv/en/publications/mechanisms-of-weight-gain-associated-with-valproic-acid-a-scoping/); [MDPI Biomedicines 2023;11(5):1499](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9059/11/5/1499); [PubMed 18583434](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/18583434)
- ASF Behavior Series case: a young woman with AS improved on **quetiapine (Seroquel)** after slow up-titration [ANEK/clinical vignette] — [ASF Behavior Series "Mental Health Influences on Aggression"](https://angelman.org/mental-health-aggression)

### Inferences
- Ranking for this patient, from greatest to least appetite/weight risk (synthesised from Pillinger and the sources above): olanzapine/clozapine > quetiapine ≈ risperidone > aripiprazole (variable) > ziprasidone/lurasidone/haloperidol. Mirtazapine and valproate are also high-risk for appetite.
- If an antipsychotic becomes necessary for aggression, the more weight-neutral options and **metformin co-treatment from the start** are a rational combination (the antipsychotic-weight-gain NMAs support metformin and GLP-1 RAs). Weight, waist and appetite should be monitored before and during treatment.
- Clonidine, guanfacine, melatonin and trazodone are not typically linked to marked appetite increase (general pharmacology, not specifically sourced here). This matters when choosing drugs for sleep and hyperarousal in this patient.

### Gaps
- No primary data on SSRIs and appetite or weight in AS or ID were retrieved.
- No AS-specific data on antipsychotic-induced weight gain were found.

## 7. Constipation management compatible with GLP-1 RAs, antipsychotics and AS

### Takeaway
Constipation affects about 70–85% of people with AS regardless of genotype. GLP-1 RAs roughly double to triple constipation rates versus placebo during dose escalation, and LGIT and many psychotropics (anticholinergic or sedating ones, e.g., clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine) can worsen it. A baseline bowel regimen should be established **before** starting any of these drugs. No source specifically on macrogol was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Glassman LW, et al. (Thibert senior author) "Prevalence of gastrointestinal symptoms in Angelman syndrome." AJMG A 2017 (doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.38401, PMID 28816003):** retrospective records of 163 patients (120 MGH, 43 UNC). 141 had at least one GI symptom; **constipation 116/163 (~71%)**, GERD 72/163 (~44%). **Constipation prevalence was similar across all genetic subtypes**; upper GI symptoms were more frequent in deletion and UPD [PR, cohort] — [ERN-ITHACA PDF](https://ern-ithaca.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Glassman_Angelman_gastrointest_AJMG2017.pdf); [PubMed 28816003](https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28816003/)
- Global Angelman Syndrome Registry, 173 children and adolescents: **84% constipation**, the most common GI symptom [PR] — [Angelman Syndrome News](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/study-links-worse-angelman-gi-symptoms-problems-infancy/); [PMC9826167](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9826167)
- LGIT can worsen or trigger constipation in AS [PR] — [Angelman Syndrome News on Thibert GI study](https://angelmansyndromenews.com/news/gastrointestinal-issues-prevalence-angelman-syndrome-constipation-gastroesophageal-reflux-disease/)
- **GLP-1 RA and constipation (secondary sources only):** STEP 1 (semaglutide 2.4 mg): ~24% vs ~9% on placebo. SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide 15 mg): ~17% (placebo figures differ between sources). Constipation peaks during dose escalation and declines afterwards. Management options include slower titration, a lower maintenance dose or switching agent [SEK, commercial/telehealth blogs, figures to verify against NEJM STEP-1/SURMOUNT-1] — [eMed UK](https://emed.com/uk/blog/health/does-semaglutide-cause-constipation); [Doctronic](https://www.doctronic.ai/blog/can-semaglutide-cause-constipation/)

### Inferences
- With hard stools and an exercise-averse, carbohydrate-selective eater, a standing bowel regimen should come before any GLP-1 RA, antipsychotic or LGIT: macrogol as first-line osmotic laxative, adequate fluids, gradual fibre increase within accepted foods, and a toilet routine. This is standard practice; no specific source was retrieved in this session.
- In non-verbal patients, constipation can itself cause pain-related agitation, aggression and sleep disruption. Treating constipation may therefore reduce behavioural symptoms, and worsening constipation on a new drug could be misread as "behaviour getting worse".

### Gaps
- No primary source for macrogol in AS, or for macrogol in combination with GLP-1 RAs, was retrieved. The German S2k guideline on chronic constipation (DGVS/DGNM) was not searched.
- Original STEP-1/SURMOUNT-1 constipation figures were not verified.

## 8. Caregiver and parent experience reports on psychotropic and sleep medications in AS (surveys = peer-reviewed caregiver perception; community reports = anecdotal)

### Takeaway
Published caregiver surveys and retrospective cohorts consistently name **melatonin** (most used, often helpful), **clonidine** (second most common, rated helpful in a QoL survey) and **trazodone** as the main sleep drugs; in one cohort they had similar response rates and were usually continued for ≥6 months. **Mirtazapine** helps sleep but causes hyperphagia and weight gain. Caregivers rate **CBD** helpful (mainly for seizures, some for mood or sleep). Clinicians report **stimulant intolerance** in hyperactive AS children, and **buspirone** has a small positive case series for anxiety-related behaviour. The ASF describes SSRIs/anxiolytics as often effective and atypical antipsychotics as helpful in rare severe aggression. **No usable Reddit, forum or Angelman e.V. testimonials about risperidone, aripiprazole, guanfacine or SSRIs could be retrieved.**

### Cited Findings

**8a. Peer-reviewed caregiver surveys and clinical cohorts (perceived effectiveness; not controlled efficacy)**
- **Pereira JA, et al. AJMG A 2020, doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.61642, PMID 32506735, "Characterization of sleep habits and medication outcomes for sleep disturbance in individuals with Angelman syndrome":** retrospective, 50 clinically referred individuals (MGH/Lurie). **72% had ever taken a sleep medication, most often melatonin, clonidine and trazodone.** "Many demonstrated significant improvement in sleep disturbances, with **no difference in odds of improvement between medications**". Most continued for **≥6 months** (tolerability proxy). Sleep disturbance was worse in younger participants [PR, retrospective cohort] — [PubMed 32506735](https://www.pubmed.gov/32506735/); [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/1e1ce963-6a53-49e0-b683-850d4989e0ac)

### Inferences
- For this patient's fragmented sleep, the caregiver and clinical data favour **melatonin** (first-line, low risk), with **clonidine** or **trazodone** as next steps (similar response rates in the Pereira cohort). **Mirtazapine should be avoided** because of hyperphagia and weight gain in AS. Clonidine (or guanfacine) may also help daytime hyperarousal or ADHD-like restlessness without raising appetite. This is a reasonable synthesis, but the ADHD use in AS is unverified.
- Stimulants are often poorly tolerated in AS (Keary 2022 title, ASF warning) and suppress appetite, which might look attractive for weight but is unpredictable. They need specialist supervision.
- For attention-seeking aggression, the ASF recommends functional behaviour assessment first (behavioural strategies), with medication as an adjunct. Because aggression is often anxiety-driven in AS, buspirone or SSRIs are plausible weight-neutral options before antipsychotics. This relies on small case series and expert opinion.
- Caregiver ratings ("helpful") come from self-selected online samples and are biased toward positive reports. In the German document they should be labelled as "Erfahrungsberichte, kein Wirksamkeitsnachweis" (experience reports, not proof of efficacy).
