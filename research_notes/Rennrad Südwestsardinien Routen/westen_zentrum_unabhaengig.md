# Road cycling (Rennrad) in central-west, north-west and inland central-south Sardinia, regardless of base town (northeast excluded)

> Method note: komoot, bikeclimbs, epicroadrides, sawadee and italia.it could not be fetched directly. The egress proxy refused the connection or DNS failed. So the figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages (komoot, Climbfinder, Wikiloc, Outdooractive, tour operators). The distance and elevation figures are user-generated or from operators and are approximate. Check the GPX before riding. Research date: Oct 2026.

## 1. Which regions are regarded as the best for road cycling, and why?

### Takeaway
Sources agree on two groups. The **Alghero–Bosa coast road (SP105)** is the island's headline ride. For quiet, technically good roads, the inland mountains (**Barbagia/Gennargentu, Montiferru, Marmilla/Sarcidano**) and in the east **Ogliastra** are named. The northwest (Montiferru hinterland behind Bosa) is singled out for "good roads, minimal traffic". There are no Alpine-style climbs. Sardinia scores on loneliness, landscape and many medium-length climbs.

### Cited Findings
- Barbagia roads: "quiet, technically good, and consistently set within a landscape of forest, granite, and traditional stone architecture" — [epicroadrides.com (search extract)](https://epicroadrides.com/destinations/cycling-italy/sardinia/)
- ROUVY recommends Supramonte, Barbagia and Gennargentu "for longer climbs and quieter mountain roads" — [rouvy.com Blog](https://rouvy.com/blog/sardinia-cycling-routes)
- Monte Ferru massif inland of Bosa: "a compact mountain mass with good roads, minimal traffic, and excellent local agriculture" — [epicroadrides.com / artofbicycletrips (search extract)](https://www.artofbicycletrips.com/blog/the-complete-guide-go-cycling-sardinia/)
- komoot highlight near Bosa: "Away from the busy coastal roads, you are in good hands with your racing bike here in the hilly hinterland" — [komoot Highlight](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/6656229)
- Marmilla (central-south): "plenty of quiet, country roads leading from one quaint village to the next" — [cycling-holidays-italy.com](https://cycling-holidays-italy.com/cycling-sardinia)
- Ogliastra/Tacchi (Gairo, Ussassai, Ulassai, Jerzu): roads "quiet, well-surfaced, and continuously spectacular" — [epicroadrides.com](https://epicroadrides.com/destinations/cycling-italy/sardinia/)
- Limitation: "road cyclists looking for a challenge won't find any alpine climbs or famous names". - ProCyclingUK (Mathew Mitchell) calls Alghero–Bosa (SP105) the island's headline ride. Porto Conte/Capo Caccia is "another version of the coast", and inland roads are quieter and can turn an out-and-back into a large loop — [ProCyclingUK](https://procyclinguk.com/cycling-in-sardinia-coastal-roads-wild-interiors-and-why-it-deserves-a-place-on-your-riding-list/)

  - KimKim 8-day tour with an Alghero/Bosa coast leg and a "Gennargentu Loop" with an overnight in Aritzo — [kimkim](https://kimkim.com/c/incredible-cycle-tour-of-sardinia-8-days)

### Inferences
- For a trip "without a fixed base" in the west and centre, three standout regions follow from the sources: (1) **Bosa/Planargia + Montiferru** (coast plus quiet mountain roads, short distances between them), (2) the **Gennargentu southern side/Mandrolisai** (longest climbs, highest passes, little traffic) and (3) **Sinis/Oristano** as a flat recovery area.
- The Alghero–Bosa–Montiferru–Cabras corridor is the operators' "standard product" in the west. That suggests good infrastructure (hotels, bike hire) and proven roads.

### Gaps
- No German magazine article (Tour Magazin, Roadbike) was found that ranks the regions. Cyclist and Bicisport also returned no hits.
- No traffic counts. All traffic statements are qualitative.

## 2. Planargia & Bosa (SP105 Alghero–Bosa, Montresta, Villanova Monteleone, Tresnuraghes/Magomadas)

### Takeaway
The **SP105 Alghero–Bosa** (about 42–47 km point to point, climbs to 300–400 m, rolling, no services) is Sardinia's best-known coastal road ride. Combined with the inland return via **Villanova Monteleone/Montresta**, it gives the classic loop of about 80–114 km with up to about 1,900 m of climbing. Traffic is light outside high season. Komoot riders recommend riding early in the morning.

### Cited Findings
- italia.it: Alghero–Bosa cycle route about 50 km, "medium" difficulty, leaves just outside the town walls along Provincial Road 105, with a final descent to Bosa — [italia.it](https://www.italia.it/en/sardinia/things-to-do/coastal-cycle-road-alghero-bosa-sardinia)
- Distances vary by source: ProCyclingUK about 60 km, other guides about 45 or about 47 km, one operator 42 km. Start and end points differ — [ProCyclingUK](https://procyclinguk.com/cycling-in-sardinia-coastal-roads-wild-interiors-and-why-it-deserves-a-place-on-your-riding-list/) (with search extracts from other guides)
- komoot: "fantastic asphalted panoramic road". Continuous ups and downs, climbs to about 300–400 m, then down to Bosa Marina. No infrastructure. Tip: ride early in the morning to avoid traffic — [komoot Highlight 3955551](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/3955551)
- bikeclimbs "SP105 Climb": about 2.6 km, up to about 11–12% in places (exact elevation gain could not be read) — [bikeclimbs](https://bikeclimbs.com/climb/italy/sardinia/sassari/alghero/sp105-climb)
- Liberty Rider (motorcycles) calls Alghero–Bosa "the most spectacular road in Sardinia" — [liberty-rider.com](https://liberty-rider.com/en/roadbooks/italia/15123/alghero-bosa-the-most-spectacular-road-in-sardinia)
- komoot SmartTour "Alghero-Bosa Scenic Road and Montresta Loop": 82.8 km, about 6 hours, "difficult". Highlight: the stretch at Villanova Monteleone with a panoramic descent over the Gulf of Alghero — [komoot](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e1040391765/alghero-bosa-scenic-road-and-montresta-loop)
- komoot "Alghero Coastal Road – Villanova Monteleone Loop": 41.9 km, about 2:56 h, with a climb to Villanova Monteleone — [komoot](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e1019084449/sulla-alghero-bose-con-ritorno-sulla-costa-giro-ad-anello)
- Outdooractive "Ascent to Villanova via Bosa": loop of about 113.9 km, about 1,900 m of climbing, "difficult", from Lido San Giovanni (Alghero) via Bosa, Montresta and Villanova Monteleone — [Outdooractive Bosa](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/cycle-routes/bosa/cycling-in-bosa/9480999/)
- komoot "Bosa Old Town – Alghero–Bosa Coastal Road loop": 72.1 km, about 1,420 m, "expert" (updated Sept 2025) — [komoot Bosa guide](https://www.komoot.com/guide/584194/itineraires-et-pistes-cyclables-autour-de-bosa)
- Montresta–Bosa descent: "the magnificent long drop down to Montresta: 9 km towards the sea, as far as Bosa" (operator description) — [Dolcevita Bike Tours "Coral Coast"](https://dolcevitabiketours.com/en/leisure-cycling/self-guided/the-coral-coast/) / [cicloposse.com](https://cicloposse.com/sardinia-bike-tour)
- komoot collections of road rides around Alghero — [komoot road cycling Alghero](https://www.komoot.com/guide/609948/itineraires-a-velo-de-course-autour-de-l-alguer-alghero)

### Inferences
- Base towns: **Bosa** (central for SP105, Montresta and Montiferru) or **Alghero** (SP105 plus Nurra/Capo Caccia). The classic route is about 80 km from Alghero (SP105 → Bosa → Montresta → Villanova Monteleone → Alghero). With the extension, it is about 114 km and 1,900 m.
- Because there are no services on the SP105, carry plenty of water, especially in summer.

### Gaps
- **Tresnuraghes–Magomadas** (Planargia south of Bosa, towards Cuglieri/SS292): no specific road-cycling source found. Only its position is clear (between Bosa and the Montiferru).
- No exact elevation figures for the SP105 point-to-point route, and no current information on surface damage.

## 3. Oristano province: Sinis, Cabras, Arborea, Monte Arci, Montiferru, Lago Omodeo/Barigadu

### Takeaway
**Montiferru** (Santu Lussurgiu–Cuglieri–Seneghe–Bonarcado, SP19/SS292) is the best road area in the province: a loop of about 68 km and about 1,000 m, climbing to about 900 m through oak and holly forest. **Sinis** (SP6, SP1) is flat, about 77 km and about 130 m of climbing, good for recovery days. **Lago Omodeo/Barigadu** has quiet, mostly paved hilly loops with short, steep ramps (Sorradile). **Monte Arci** is mostly MTB and gravel, not a road area.

### Cited Findings
**Montiferru**
- Wikiloc "Shardana Bike – Montiferru loop from Santu Lussurgiu": about 68 km and about 1,000 m (the track data say about 3,448 ft, about 1,050 m). First down via Bonarcado and Seneghe, then a long climb to about 900 m past Cuglieri through oak and holly forest, then a long descent to Santu Lussurgiu — [Wikiloc 6392853](https://www.wikiloc.com/cycling-trails/shardana-bike-montiferru-loop-from-santu-lussurgiu-68km-1000m-6392853)
- Route Cuglieri → Santu Lussurgiu: SS292 (Corso Umberto I) then SP19 towards Santu Lussurgiu. Mix of straights and panoramic bends (motorcycle source) — [trueriders.it](https://www.trueriders.it/itinerari-moto/itinerari-moto-italia/sardegna-in-moto/nascosta-tra-i-fitti-boschi-del-montiferru-ce-una-cascata-segreta-che-regala-unoasi-di-pace-incontaminata/)
- Operator stage Cuglieri → Santu Lussurgiu: "long climb in the shadow of an oak forest". Day figures: 48 km, 1,100 m, about 3.5 h — [sardiniacycling.com](https://www.sardiniacycling.com/en/biking-tours/the-west-coast-of-sardinia-on-e-bike.html)
- Local club ASD Montiferru MTB (Cuglieri, Santu Lussurgiu, Seneghe), a contact for road conditions — [montiferrumtb.it](https://www.montiferrumtb.it/en/home/)
- piste-ciclabili.com: on the west-coast route, steep climbs on the Montiferru slopes beyond Bosa — [piste-ciclabili.com](https://www.piste-ciclabili.com/itinerari/3679-costa-ovest-della-sardegna)

**Sinis / Cabras**
- Access: SS131 (exit Oristano Nord–Siamaggiore) → SS292 → SP1, or via Oristano → SP81 → SP1. The **SP6** runs down the peninsula to San Giovanni di Sinis (Tharros) — [Wikivoyage DE San Giovanni di Sinis](https://de.wikivoyage.org/wiki/San_Giovanni_di_Sinis)

- Bikemap/Girolibero loop Cabras – Tharros – Is Arutas – Putzu Idu – Riola Sardo – Cabras: about 77 km, about 128 m of climbing (the pairing of the figures is not certain) — [Bikemap](https://www.bikemap.net/en/r/115043)

**Arborea / Oristano flatlands**

### Inferences
- Base towns: **Santu Lussurgiu** or **Cuglieri** (Montiferru, mountain village), **Cabras/Oristano** (Sinis + Arborea, flat), **Ghilarza** (Omodeo/Barigadu, gateway to the Mandrolisai). Santa Caterina di Pittinuri is on the SS292 coastal stretch between Cuglieri and Riola. It is a possible base for Montiferru plus Sinis, but no specific cycling source was found for it.
- Monte Arci is better treated as gravel/MTB terrain. The asphalt around it (Marmilla villages) is better suited to road riding.

### Gaps

- Seneghe – Santu Lussurgiu specific climb data (length/gradient) not found.
- Arborea: no specific road-cycling loops with figures found (only the passage on the west-coast route).

## 4. Nurra / Alghero west: Capo Caccia, Porto Ferro, Argentiera, Stintino

### Takeaway
The Nurra is largely flat to rolling. Capo Caccia from Alghero is about 51 km and about 115 m of climbing. Stintino → Capo Caccia is about 54 km (SP69 mostly downhill to the coast). Big minus: **summer traffic** on the beach roads (Capo Caccia, the last 10 km before Stintino, main roads at Porto Torres). Quieter alternatives run via SP57/Palmadula. No water in between.

### Cited Findings
- Bike Alghero: Capo Caccia ride mostly flat, 50.8 km, 115 m of climbing. The last 10 km before Stintino are "not recommended during the high season due to heavy traffic" — [bikealghero.com](https://www.bikealghero.com/sardinia-cycling/)
- The road Capo Caccia–Alghero is busier on summer weekends because of beach traffic — [bikealghero.com / Italy Cycling Guide (search extract)](https://italy-cycling-guide.info/cycle-routes/coastal-rides/sardegna-north-coast/part-5/)
- Italy Cycling Guide "Sardegna North Coast Part 5: Stintino to Alghero": Stintino → Capo Caccia about 54 km. The SP69 is "mainly downhill all the way to the coast" — [italy-cycling-guide.info](https://italy-cycling-guide.info/cycle-routes/coastal-rides/sardegna-north-coast/part-5/)
- komoot Asinara/Stintino guide: the direct road west from the Stintino junction is "not recommended for cyclists due to heavy traffic and dangerous speeds". The old provincial road is longer, more scenic and quieter — [komoot Asinara guide](https://www.komoot.com/guide/503851/fantastici-percorsi-per-bici-da-corsa-nei-dintorni-del-parco-nazionale-dell-asinara)
- Life in Travel "Nurra in bici" (Porto Torres – Alghero): quiet roads via the **SP57** towards Palmadula (water there). No supplies along the way — [lifeintravel.it](https://www.lifeintravel.it/viaggi/italia-in-bicicletta/nurra-in-bici-in-sardegna-tra-porto-torres-e-alghero.html)
- Sardegnaturismo "Il Capo di Sopra su due ruote" (northern Sardinia by bike) — [sardegnaturismo.it](https://www.sardegnaturismo.it/it/il-capo-di-sopra-su-due-ruote)
- Porto Ferro appears in the sources mainly in MTB contexts (routes to Argentiera and Capo Caccia) — [CAI Sassari Stintino](https://www.caisassari.it/cicloescursioni/stintino/); [waterwind.it](https://www.waterwind.it/new/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1423%3Aalghero-e-stintino-sardegna-in-mtb&catid=391&lang=it)

### Inferences
- The Nurra is best in spring and autumn, or early in the morning. In July and August, the beach roads (Capo Caccia, Stintino) are a negative factor.
- A combined Alghero base works best: SP105/Bosa for climbing, Nurra for flat and rolling days.

### Gaps
- No current traffic data for Porto Ferro – Argentiera (asphalt link via Palmadula). No surface information.

## 5. Central inland: Gennargentu southern side, Mandrolisai, Barbagia di Belvì, Sarcidano (plus a short Ogliastra note)

### Takeaway
This is the island's **climbing region**: **Passo Tascusì** (1,247 m, very little traffic), **Bruncu Spina** (1,554 m, the highest asphalt road on the island in the region), and **S'Arena** (1,507 m). Loops such as Laconi–Meana–Atzara–Sorgono–Tonara–Belvì–Aritzo–Laconi (about 87 km, about 1,850 m) run on mostly good asphalt with little traffic. Fonni (about 1,000 m) is Sardinia's highest municipality.

### Cited Findings
- Climbfinder **Passo Tascusì from Tonara**: 13.6 km, 397 m, average 2.9%, steepest 100 m at 16.5%, top 1,247 m. A reviewer reports very little traffic — [Climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/passo-tascusi-tonara)
- Tascusì via **SP7** from Desulo towards Fonni. The second-highest car pass in Sardinia — [komoot Highlight 5438761 (search extract)](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/5438761); [bikeclimbs Tascusì](https://bikeclimbs.com/climb/italy/sardinia/nuoro/desulu-desulo/tascusi)
- Climbfinder **Bruncu Spina from Fonni**: 14.3 km, 605 m, average 4.2%, top 1,554 m — [Climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/bruncu-spina-fonni-onne)
- Climbfinder **S'Arena from Passo Tascusì**: 6.1 km, 285 m, average 4.7%, top 1,507 m. Final hairpins below the refuge about 15%, surface "fair" — [Climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/s-arena-passo-tascusi)
- komoot "Bruncu Spina from Desulo": 72.0 km, 1,720 m, about 4:07 h, hard, mostly well paved — [komoot Aritzo guide](https://www.komoot.com/guide/608843/road-cycling-routes-around-aritzu-aritzo)
- Bruncu Spina access road branches off a little over 5 km past Fonni — [Transfeero Blog](https://www.transfeero.com/de/reiseblog/punta-la-marmora-gennargentu-on-sardinias-roof/); Fonni about 1,000 m, the highest municipality — [Touring Club](https://www.touringclub.it/destinazioni/fonni/vedere/274693-gennargentu)

- **SS128**: Monastir – Laconi – Meana Sardo – Sorgono – Tonara, 164 km in total. ## 6. Pro races and Granfondos on these roads

### Takeaway
The Giro used Sardinia as its Grande Partenza in 1961, 2007 and 2017. **2007** had a stage finish in **Bosa** (Tempio–Bosa, McEwen) and **Barumini → Cagliari**. In **2017**, stage 1 started in **Alghero**, but it ran along the north coast to Olbia, not the SP105. Stage 2 crossed **Genna Silana**. The Granfondo with west Sardinia connections is the **Andalas – Strade Sarde** (Alghero, 136 km, 2,500 m, strade-bianche style with gravel and cobbles). Earlier GiroSardegna stages also used Alghero–Bosa.
