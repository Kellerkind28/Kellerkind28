# Rennrad-Routen im Umkreis 50–100 km von Punta Trettu (Süd- & Westsardinien)

> Method note: WebFetch and direct page access were blocked in this research environment (proxy refused komoot.com, italia.it, pedalo.com). Every finding below comes from search-engine result snippets and summaries of the linked pages, not from full page reads. Figures (km, Hm) are taken from those snippets and should be checked on the live pages. Driving distances from Punta Trettu are **my own estimates from regional geography (no source)** and appear only in the Inferences sections.

## 1. Costa del Sud: Teulada–Chia (SP71), Domus de Maria, Pula, Santa Margherita, plus inland climbs (Santadi, Pantaleo, Is Cannoneris, Gutturu Mannu)

### Takeaway
The SP71 "Strada panoramica della Costa del Sud" (about 25 km, Teulada/Porto di Teulada to Chia) is the signature road ride of the south. It is a rolling, non-trivial coast road with good asphalt. Is Cannoneris from Domus de Maria (9.3 km at 6.6%) is the best-documented real climb in the area. Avoid the SS195 between Pula and Chia: local press calls it dangerous for cyclists. The SP1 Capoterra–Gutturu Mannu–Santadi is largely gravel and is NOT a road-bike route.

### Cited Findings
**SP71 Costa del Sud (Teulada–Chia)**
- A komoot highlight describes the SP71 as "a fantastic panoramic road… about 25 km long… one of the best known and most panoramic roads in the Sardinian cycling panorama." Its main viewpoints are Piscinni, Capo Malfatano and Tuerredda, with "a succession of ascents and descents that are by no means trivial." — [komoot highlight 109204](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/109204)
- Another komoot highlight covers Teulada–Domus de Maria. It calls the road "spectacular with views of the sea and mountains," with "fun climbs and descents in both directions." One rider describes an "easy (4–6%) mountain, very good road, winding climb… especially in the early morning hours. Great, curved descent to Domus de Maria." — [komoot highlight 5367278](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/5367278); [komoot highlight 819255](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/819255)
- komoot rates the "Teulada to Domus de Maria" ring tour as hard: 33.8 km, 750 Hm, about 3:03 h. — [komoot (search snippet)](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/5647498)
- Italia.it describes a 50 km loop Chia–Domus de Maria–Teulada, rated medium. It climbs over the Nuraxi de Mesu pass and descends through eucalyptus and pine forest to Domus de Maria. — [Italia.it – The South Coast cycle path](https://www.italia.it/en/sardinia/things-to-do/the-south-coast-cycle-path)
- One source describes the SP71 between Chia and Porto di Teulada as narrow and winding, "difficult to pass when two vehicles meet." This is a traffic and safety concern, not a statement about traffic volume. — [Slowroads – Sardinia South Coast Panorama (SP71)](https://www.slowroads.eu/en/route/sardinia-south-coast)
- A route guide says the stretch Sant'Anna Arresi–Teulada is about 13 km. It switches from the SS195 to the SP71 to return to the sea. — [Abamar Hotel – 4 itinerari in bici nel Sud Sardegna](https://www.abamarhotel.net/blog/4-itinerari-in-bici-nel-sud-sardegna/)
- That guide gives Pula→Teulada as about 33 km, "slightly more difficult," via Chia and Torre di Chia. — [Abamar Hotel](https://www.abamarhotel.net/blog/4-itinerari-in-bici-nel-sud-sardegna/)
- komoot has a "Southern coast of Sardinia from Chia" loop of 48.4 km, with a warning that some segments may be unpaved. Bikemap's "Sa Tuerra – Costa del Sud loop" lists the longest climb as about 6 km after Chia, where the route turns inland. — [komoot smarttour e1013220620](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e1013220620/southern-coast-of-sardinia-from-chia-loop-tour); [Bikemap route 3513213](https://www.bikemap.net/en/r/3513213/)
- A komoot loop from Pula to the Torre di Chia viewpoint is about 39 km with about 230 Hm, rated moderate. — [komoot collection (search snippet)](https://www.komoot.com/nl-nl/collection/2792668/sardinia-by-road-bike-ten-routes-to-discover-the-island)
- Flyedelweiss lists a hard Pula route of about 158 km with about 1,390 Hm. It goes inland through the Is Cannoneris forests to Teulada and returns along the south coast. — [Flyedelweiss – Road Cycling Sardinia](https://flyedelweiss.com/de/en/destinations/tips/road-cycling/sardinia.html)
- A komoot route from Chia to Porto Pino follows the seaside of the municipality of Teulada. — [komoot guide 62409 – road cycling Sud Sardegna](https://www.komoot.com/guide/62409/road-cycling-routes-in-sud-sardegna)

**Inland climbs**
- Climbfinder ranks the "Strada Montana per Is Cannoneris" from Domus de Maria as the 3rd-best climb in South Sardinia: 9.3 km, 6.6% average, 541 points. — [Climbfinder – Cycling climbs South Sardinia](https://climbfinder.com/en/regions/sud-sardegna)
- Is Cannoneris covers about 4,800 ha across Domus de Maria, Pula, Teulada and Villa San Pietro. Its highest point is Punta Sebera at 979 m. — [Sardegna Turismo – Is Cannoneris](https://www.sardegnaturismo.it/it/esplora/cannoneris)
- I found no Climbfinder climb for Santadi, Pantaleo or Teulada. The only Pantaleo routes found are MTB tracks on Wikiloc (e.g. Pantaleo–Is Cannoneris, about 21.7 km and about 945 Hm, rated difficult). — [Wikiloc Pantaleo MTB](https://www.wikiloc.com/trails/mountain-biking/italy/sardinia/pantaleo)
- An older guide (about 10 years old) describes a 53 km mixed route from south of Domus de Maria past Capo Teulada to Santadi: "isolated and wild landscape with plenty of hills." — [Rouvy / komoot snippets via search](https://rouvy.com/blog/sardinia-cycling-routes)
- A komoot collection includes a route from Santadi toward Teulada. Details were not visible in the snippet. — [komoot collection 2792668](https://www.komoot.com/nl-nl/collection/2792668/sardinia-by-road-bike-ten-routes-to-discover-the-island)

**SS195 Pula–Chia: avoid or minimise**

### Inferences
- The best quiet core loop is Teulada → SP71 coast → Chia → inland over the Nuraxi de Mesu saddle to Domus de Maria → back to Teulada. It is about 50 km with 700–900 Hm and uses the SS195 only briefly near Teulada and Domus de Maria. The Hm range is interpolated from the 33.8 km/750 Hm and 50 km "medium" figures.
- Driving estimates, my own and unsourced: Teulada about 35–40 km (about 40 min) from Punta Trettu, via SS195 through Giba and Masainas. Chia about 60 km (about 1 h). Domus de Maria about 55 km. Pula and Santa Margherita about 70–80 km. Santadi about 30 km. Teulada and Santadi are therefore below the 50 km ring but are the natural start points for the Costa del Sud.
- Traffic on the SP71 is very likely seasonal: beach traffic to Tuerredda and Cala Cipolla in July–August. That fits the general Rouvy remark below and the early-morning advice above. Not verified with counts.
- To add an inland climb from Teulada, ride toward Santadi and Pantaleo on minor roads. No reliable road-bike source was found for these, so I can't confirm surface or traffic.

### Gaps
- No traffic counts or recent rider reports specifically on SP71 traffic volume.
- No road-bike climb profiles for Teulada–Santadi, Santadi–Pantaleo or the Sarroch/Villa San Pietro hinterland. The Pantaleo-area results were MTB-only.
- I couldn't confirm whether the access road to the Capo Spartivento lighthouse is paved.
- No source on the Sarroch hinterland as a road-bike area. The refinery zone along the SS195 there is presumably busy, but that is unverified.

## 2. Costa Verde / Iglesiente / Arburese: Nebida–Masua–Buggerru–Portixeddu–Capo Pecora (SP83), Fluminimaggiore–Arbus (SS126, Genna Bogai), Montevecchio, Piscinas, Guspini, Gonnosfanadiga / Monte Linas

### Takeaway
This is the most climbing-rich coastal area within reach. The SP83 coast road (Nebida–Masua–Buggerru) has the Passo Montecani climbs. The SS126 crosses Genna Bogai on the Fluminimaggiore side (12.7 km at 3.9%, described as low-traffic) and Passo Bidderdi (492 m) toward Arbus. Montevecchio is reachable on asphalt. Piscinas is NOT: the Ingurtosu–Piscinas access and the Piscinas–Costa Verde road are unpaved. Perd'e Pibera on Monte Linas is paved for only about 5 km and then gets extremely steep and paved-stone or gravel.

### Cited Findings
**SP83 Nebida–Masua–Buggerru–Portixeddu–Capo Pecora**
- A cycling trip report describes the SP83 from Buggerru past Portixeddu along a scenic road down to Capo Pecora. It gives the Masua–Buggerru climb as about 4.5 km from 134 m to the 437 m Monte Cani pass, "~12%" average. That gradient figure looks inconsistent: 303 m over 4.5 km is about 6.7%. Climbfinder's numbers below are more plausible. — [yogalpinismo – Ciclovia Cammino Minerario di Santa Barbara (2024)](https://www.yogalpinismo.altervista.org/2024/20241231CMSB/relazione.html); [Italy Cycling Guide – Sardegna West Coast Part 6](https://italy-cycling-guide.info/cycle-routes/coastal-rides/sardegna-west-coast/part-6/)
- Climbfinder lists Passo Montecani variants: from Spiaggia Portu Cauli 4.5 km at 8.9% (435 pts), from Fontanamare 9 km at 5.1% (413 pts), from Cala Domestica 6.9 km at 5.8% (290 pts). — [Climbfinder – South Sardinia](https://climbfinder.com/en/regions/sud-sardegna); [Climbfinder ranking](https://climbfinder.com/us/ranking?l=789)
- komoot lists 24 road-bike tours around Buggerru, updated November 2025. The top one is about 41 km with about 1,080 Hm via the Nebida road, rated hard, mostly good asphalt. — [komoot – Rennradtouren rund um Buggerru](https://www.komoot.com/de-de/guide/570763/rennradtouren-rund-um-bugerru-buggerru)
- Route guides say the approach from Iglesias is SS126 toward Sant'Antioco, then SP83: about 11 km to the Nebida/Masua junction, about 12 km more to the Masua intersection. — search summary of [Italy Cycling Guide Part 6](https://italy-cycling-guide.info/cycle-routes/coastal-rides/sardegna-west-coast/part-6/)
- I found no source on SP83 surface quality or current traffic.

**SS126 Fluminimaggiore–Genna Bogai / Arbus–Passo Bidderdi**
- Climbfinder lists Passo Genna Bogai from Fluminimaggiore at 12.7 km, 3.9% average, 3rd category. Its reviewer says it is largely tree-sheltered, with asphalt in good condition and little traffic. — [Climbfinder – Passo Genna Bogai from Fluminimaggiore](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/passo-genna-bogai-fluminimaggiore)
- Other sources give different lengths: about 6.1 km at 4.8% to the pass at 557 m, or about 8 km at just under 5%. The start points probably differ. — [Climbfinder (IT)](https://climbfinder.com/it/salite/passo-genna-bogai-fluminimaggiore); [tuttobiciweb – Settimana Ciclistica Italiana](https://www.tuttobiciweb.it/article/1626362459)
- Live-traffic entries for the SS126 dated June 2026 report a landslide and roadworks between Arcu Genna Bogai and Fluminimaggiore. Current status is unknown, so check before riding. — [motorionline – Traffico SS126](https://www.motorionline.com/traffico/autostrada/SS126/)
- A guided-route description warns that you may meet traffic on the SS126 when returning to Iglesias. This is anecdotal. — search summary of [Cammino Minerario Santa Barbara – Tappa 01 bici](https://www.camminominerariodisantabarbara.org/it/resource/statictrack/tappa-01-bici-montana/)
- Passo Bidderdi (492 m) is on the SS126 between Arbus and Fluminimaggiore. quaeldich.de says that starting from the sea (Portixeddu) means at least 500 Hm. A komoot tip says there are no water points, so fill bottles first. — [quaeldich.de – Passo Bidderdi](https://www.quaeldich.de/paesse/passo-bidderdi/); [komoot – Top 7 Pässe Südsardinien](https://www.komoot.com/guide/3892066/paesse-in-suedsardinien)
- komoot calls the Bidderdi road "scenic, winding, and well-asphalted… a favorite among motorcyclists and road cyclists." Expect motorbikes at weekends; that part is my inference. — [komoot – Arbus attractions](https://www.komoot.com/guide/629672/los-mejores-sitios-que-ver-en-arbus)
- komoot loops include one over Bidderdi at 127.1 km with 1,500+ Hm, and a "Costa Verde and Montevecchio" loop at 88.8 km with 1,000+ Hm. — [komoot – Top 7 Pässe Südsardinien](https://www.komoot.com/guide/3892066/paesse-in-suedsardinien)
- Climbfinder also lists "Passo Bidderdi from Monte Cidro," about 7.7 km (4.8 mi) at 5.4%. The snippet was garbled and the side is unclear. — [Climbfinder ranking](https://climbfinder.com/us/ranking?l=789)

**Arbus–Montevecchio–Ingurtosu–Piscinas–Torre dei Corsari**
- The Italy Cycling Guide (about 7 years old) says most of the Montevecchio section is surfaced, climbing from sea level to about 450 m with no really big climbs. Montevecchio sits at about 370 m. It reports unsurfaced road between Ingurtosu and Piscinas beach and between Piscinas and Costa Verde, and recommends going via Arbus and Ingurtosu. The direct Montevecchio–Ingurtosu road is dirt and was signposted "strada chiusa." — [Italy Cycling Guide – Sardegna West Coast Part 5](https://italy-cycling-guide.info/cycle-routes/coastal-rides/sardegna-west-coast/part-5/)
- Sardegna Turismo lists Pistis and Torre dei Corsari as the first coastal spots of the Arbus area, followed by Marina di Arbus, Piscinas and Scivu. At Piscinas the route turns inland through the mining landscape. — [Sardegna Turismo – Cycling freely through enchanting landscapes](https://www.sardegnaturismo.it/en/cycling-freely-through-enchanting-landscapes)
- komoot has a separate gravel guide for Arbus, which suggests much of the Piscinas/Ingurtosu network is gravel. — [komoot – gravel rides around Arbus](https://www.komoot.com/guide/3466016/percorsi-gravel-nei-dintorni-di-arbus)
- Travelstride lists a guided Costa Verde tour (hybrid bikes, not road bikes) based near Arbus, visiting the Montevecchio mines. — [Travelstride – Explore & Cycle Sardinia](https://www.travelstride.com/trips/explore-cycle-sardinia)

**Gonnosfanadiga / Monte Linas**
- Climbfinder lists Perd'e Pibera from Gonnosfanadiga at about 4.8 km (3 mi), 4.5% average, max 13.6%, about 214 Hm (702 ft), 4th category. — [Climbfinder – Perd'e Pibera](https://climbfinder.com/us/climbs/perd-e-pibera-gonnosfanadiga)
- Wikiloc tracks say the road is asphalt up to the park, then paved-stone and steep inside it (over 20%, one track says 24–27%), then trail with walking sections. Not suitable beyond the park for a road bike. — [Wikiloc – Periplo del Monte Linas](https://www.wikiloc.com/mountain-biking-trails/periplo-del-monte-linas-da-perda-e-pibera-a-genna-eidadi-togoro-e-santa-severa-67231277); [Wikiloc – Gonnosfanadiga Perd'e Pibera](https://www.wikiloc.com/mountain-biking-trails/gonnosfanadiga-perde-pibera-non-solo-nose-press-12956519)

**Western Marmilla edge**
- Climbfinder lists "Giara di Siddi via Siddi" as a road climb. Details were not in the snippet. — [Climbfinder – Giara di Siddi](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/giara-di-siddi-siddi)

### Inferences
- A strong Costa Verde loop: Fluminimaggiore → SS126 over Genna Bogai → back via Buggerru/SP83, or Fluminimaggiore → Bidderdi → Arbus → Guspini/Montevecchio → back. The komoot 88.8 km / 1,000+ Hm "Costa Verde and Montevecchio" loop fits this.
- Driving estimates, my own and unsourced: Nebida/Masua about 35–40 km, Buggerru about 50 km, Fluminimaggiore about 45–50 km, Arbus about 75 km, Guspini about 75–80 km, Gonnosfanadiga about 70 km. All go via Carbonia/Iglesias on the SS126.
- Piscinas: leave it out for road bikes, or treat it as an out-and-back to the end of the asphalt.
- Traffic judgements here are anecdotal: one Climbfinder reviewer for Genna Bogai, one guide for the SS126 near Iglesias. The SS126 is the main north–south axis of the area, so expect more traffic near Iglesias than in the mountain section.

### Gaps
- No surface or traffic data for the SP83 or for the Portixeddu–Capo Pecora spur.
- Current SS126 landslide and roadworks status between Genna Bogai and Fluminimaggiore (June 2026 entries) is unresolved.
- No source confirming whether the Torre dei Corsari access road is fully paved.
- No road-bike source for a Guspini–Montevecchio climb profile.

## 3. Marmilla / Giara / Sarcidano / Trexenta (Barumini, Tuili, Gesturi, Villanovaforru, Lunamatrona, Laconi, Senorbì)

### Takeaway
This is a network of quiet, mostly well-paved secondary roads in gentle hills, with the Giara plateau as a backdrop. The Giara top itself is gravel or rock, MTB only. Good documented starting points are Villanovaforru (25+ komoot road routes, including an 88 km Sardara–Barumini loop) and Laconi (44 km/950 Hm and 129 km routes). The drive is long (about 1.5–2 h). Laconi is likely beyond 100 km.

### Cited Findings
- komoot lists 25+ road routes around Villanovaforru. A popular loop starts in Sàrdara, passes Su Nuraxi at Barumini, and is about 88 km (about 4 h). Some routes mix paved and unpaved. — [komoot – Rennradtouren rund um Villanovaforru](https://www.komoot.com/guide/570814/rennradtouren-rund-um-biddanoa-de-forru-villanovaforru)
- Biketoursardinia runs guided one-day Marmilla rides of 40–100 km. One goes Sanluri → Barumini → climb toward Villanovaforru → back via Lunamatrona. — [Biketoursardinia – Marmilla one-day ride](https://www.biketoursardinia.com/tour/sardinia-cycling-marmilla-one-day-ride/); [Biketoursardinia – Destination one-day ride](https://www.biketoursardinia.com/tour/sardinia-cycling-destination-one-day-ride/)
- Bikemap shows about 18 community routes near Barumini, including Barumini–Mandas at about 94 km/1,361 Hm. They are unverified. — [Bikemap – Barumini](https://www.bikemap.net/it/l/2525589/)
- A komoot Laconi–Parco Aymerich loop is 44.4 km/950 Hm, expert level, mostly paved. — [komoot (search snippet)](https://www.komoot.com/it-it/highlight/6915390)

Not for road bikes. — [CAI Cagliari – Il Parco della Giara (2022)](https://www.caicagliari.it/cicloescursionismo/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2022/09/2022_08_02-Descrizione-cicloescursione-Il-Parco-della-Giara.pdf)

### Inferences
- A practical Marmilla day: drive to Villanovaforru or Sardara (estimated about 85–95 km, about 1:10–1:20 h, mostly via SS130/SS131, my estimate). Then loop Villanovaforru–Lunamatrona–Tuili–Barumini–Gesturi (the road around the Giara foot) and back. That is about 60–90 km in rolling terrain.
- Estimated drives: Barumini about 105–110 km. Laconi about 130–140 km, beyond the 100 km brief. Senorbì about 110 km.
- The Marmilla's quiet reputation rests on general statements (Epic Road Rides, komoot), not counts. Treat it as anecdotal but consistent across sources.

## 4. Cagliari hinterland and Sarrabus (south-east): Sinnai–Burcei, old SS125 / Sette Fratelli, Villasimius–Costa Rei SP17, Castiadas

### Takeaway
The old SS125 from San Gregorio (Sinnai) to Arcu 'e Tidu, now NSA 371, is the classic quiet climb east of Cagliari because traffic moved to the new SS125. It has hairpins, a moderate gradient and a pass at about 426–430 m. Burcei is reached via the SP21. Villasimius–Costa Rei–Castiadas offers about 40–50 km coastal loops. Coastal traffic is "fairly busy" but slowed by bends. The Castiadas–Olia Speciosa straight has fast cars. All of this is a long drive from Punta Trettu (estimated 1.5–2 h through Cagliari).

### Cited Findings
- The old SS125, reclassified NSA 371, climbs hairpins from km 26 at San Gregorio (Sinnai) to near the pine wood above Burcei. Since the new SS125 was built it "has lost its importance," so traffic is light. It is described as a good road-bike route, mostly asphalt, "panoramic and very evocative," with a moderate incline and a stress-free descent. — search summary of [lamiasardegna.it – Burcei](https://www.lamiasardegna.it/burcei.htm); [Wikipedia – Cronoscalata San Gregorio-Burcei](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronoscalata_San_Gregorio-Burcei); [lifeintravel – Orientale Sarda in bici](https://www.lifeintravel.it/orientale-sarda-in-bicicletta-da-villasimius-a-olbia.html)
- The SP21 meets the SS125 at Passo di Arcu 'e Tidu (426 m). The San Gregorio–Burcei hillclimb is a car race, not a cycling race. — [Wikipedia – Cronoscalata San Gregorio-Burcei](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronoscalata_San_Gregorio-Burcei)
- A Sinnai–Burcei Wikiloc loop (about 43 km, about 1,200 Hm) is MTB with sandy sections. Don't confuse it with the road route. — [Wikiloc – Sinnai Burcei completo](https://it.wikiloc.com/percorsi-mountain-bike/sinnai-burcei-completo-44929968)

Another guide says Villasimius–Castiadas–Olia Speciosa is little used, so be self-sufficient for breakdowns. All of these are anecdotal. — [veneziabike.it – Capo Carbonara–Castiadas](http://www.veneziabike.it/capocarbonara.html); [outdooractive – cycle routes Villasimius](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/cycle-routes/villasimius/cycling-in-villasimius/201630420/)

Another komoot tour goes "From Villasimius to Costa Rei with return on the old SP20." — [komoot smarttour e1014314970](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e1014314970/giro-ad-anello-da-costa-rei-a-villasimius); [komoot smarttour e998709475](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e998709475/de-villasimius-a-costa-rei-avec-retour-sur-l-ancienne-sp20-le-tour); [komoot – Capo Carbonara](https://www.komoot.com/guide/3253060/fantastici-percorsi-e-itinerari-ciclabili-nei-dintorni-di-zona-di-protezione-speciale-capo-carbonara)

### Inferences

## 5. Roads to avoid (SS130, SS131, SS195 near Cagliari/Pula, SS554, SS126 near Iglesias) and general traffic and seasonality

### Takeaway
Only the SS195 Pula–Chia has documented recent cyclist-safety complaints (2024–2026 press). For the SS130, SS131 and SS554 I found no cycling-specific sources. Their avoidance is an inference from their role as 4-lane trunk roads. Rouvy says traffic in Sardinia is generally light compared with the mainland but coastal roads get busier in July and August.

### Cited Findings
- SS195 Pula–Chia: lane narrowing, dangerous overtaking, many cyclists (reader letter, about 2024). In 2026: root heaves and oleanders push riders off the shoulder, and the Pula mayor calls it a threat. — [L'Unione Sarda (letter)](https://www.unionesarda.it/caraunione/la-195-tra-pula-e-chia-sorpassi-pericolosi-e-troppi-ciclisti-qjcek3l9); [L'Unione Sarda 2026](https://www.unionesarda.it/news-sardegna/provincia-cagliari/radici-sotto-l-asfalto-e-oleandri-che-sporgono-l-incubo-dei-ciclisti-sulla-195-ktioblwo)
- Italian law obliges cyclists to use a bike lane where one exists. Cagliari's lanes are criticised as poorly designed. This is a political piece (Feb 2025). — [Casteddu Online](https://www.castedduonline.it/cagliari-il-flop-delle-piste-ciclabili-pericolose-e-disegnate-male-solo-lo-03-dei-cagliaritani-le-utilizza-rischio-incidenti-altissimo/)
- "Traffic is generally light compared with many mainland cycling destinations, although coastal roads naturally become busier during July and August." — [Rouvy – Sardinia cycling routes](https://rouvy.com/blog/sardinia-cycling-routes)
- Away from towns, main roads and coastal roads, secondary roads are "generally lovely and quiet." — [Epic Road Rides](https://epicroadrides.com/destinations/cycling-italy/sardinia/)
- Few bars or shops outside villages, so carry water and food. Bidderdi has no water points. — [biketrailspfannenstiel.ch – Sardinien Rennrad](https://biketrailspfannenstiel.ch/auskunft/sardinien-radfahren-rennrad/); [komoot – Pässe Südsardinien](https://www.komoot.com/guide/3892066/paesse-in-suedsardinien)

### Inferences
- The SS130 (Cagliari–Iglesias), SS131 (Carlo Felice) and SS554 (Cagliari ring) are four-lane, high-speed roads. Use them only for the car approach and never ride them. This is my own assessment; no cycling source was found.
- Quiet alternatives: on the Costa del Sud, the SP71 instead of the SS195 between Teulada and Chia. Toward Pula, the inland Domus de Maria road or the Is Cannoneris climb. In the Iglesiente, the SP83 coast and the Genna Bogai mountain section instead of the SS126 valley near Iglesias. Toward Sinnai and Burcei, the old SS125 instead of the new one.
- Best season: spring and autumn (March–May, September–October). Avoid coastal roads at midday in July and August. This is consistent with Rouvy and the early-morning SP71 advice.

### Gaps
- No cycling-specific traffic reports for the SS130, SS131 or SS554 (avoidance is inferred).
- No official traffic counts (ANAS/Provincia) for any SP road in scope.
- No Strava segment data was retrievable (Strava wasn't accessible via search snippets).
